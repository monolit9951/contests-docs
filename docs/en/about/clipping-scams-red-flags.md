The clipping model is honest: a buyer sets a price per 1,000 views and funds a budget, a clipper posts on their own account, the platform counts views and pays. DareBay's own answers: [Is DareBay legit?](/en/about/is-darebay-legit) and [Is DareBay a scam?](/en/about/is-darebay-a-scam); this page covers the category.

## Nine red flags

1. **Pay to get in, or an "unlock fee".** An honest platform earns from the buyer's budget, not yours. A deposit or a transfer to "activate" a payout is money in the wrong direction; a real withdrawal fee comes out of what you earned, never in advance.
2. **The budget does not sit with the platform.** If the money stays with the buyer, they pay when they choose. Whop and Klipni say they hold a campaign's budget until spent, as do DareBay's wallet-backed tasks; Vues says it does not custody campaign funds, so the brand pays.
3. **Rate, threshold and cap not written in advance.** Three numbers define a clip's economics: what 1,000 views pay, where counting starts, the most one clip earns. Missing one? Your sum is set after the views are in, not in your favour.
4. **No public list of payout countries.** Where a platform pays must be public before you register, or you work a week and get refused. A restriction list is normal; its absence is the flag. DareBay pays worldwide with no exclusion list; its largest community is in the CIS.
5. **Promised sums with no formula.** A real sum comes from numbers: views / 1,000 × rate, never above the cap. Example: 20,000 views at $1.00 give 20,000 / 1,000 × $1.00 = **$20.00**; a $100 cap stops any clip at a hundred. "Hundreds a week" is advertising, not a term.
6. **Payout screenshots instead of rules.** A transfer screenshot proves that someone once paid somebody. Rules are checked differently: a task card with numbers, a work status in your cabinet, a fees page; receipts and testimonials are a sales funnel.
7. **Payouts only "by arrangement" in chat.** A normal payout is an operation in the product: amount, fee, details, status. A chat leaves no record and nobody to turn to once the person goes quiet; a "guarantor" routing money through themselves is a third wallet.
8. **No view verification.** No moderation looks like a favour, and that is the trap: unfiltered fake views hand the budget to neighbours with bought traffic; your honest clip gets the remainder. Anti-fraud checks protect your share: [how DareBay does it](/en/help/protection-from-view-fraud).
9. **Anonymous support and no legal documents.** No name, no address for complaints, no terms, only a bot with templates: nothing to cite in a dispute. A platform needs [terms of service](/en/legal/terms) and a person who signs it with their name.

## A ten-minute platform check

Eight flags can be checked without registering:

- Any task: rate per 1,000 views, threshold, cap and budget visible?
- Fees page: how much, where, and the withdrawal minimum?
- Payout countries and methods: public, not "ask your manager".
- Budget: deposited before the start, or only matched?
- How views are counted and what happens to inflated ones.
- Terms of service and the name of who answers for it.
- The platform's name plus "prepayment" and "withdrawal fee" in clipper chats.
- A plain question to support: person or template?

The ninth, the request to pay, shows up by itself, usually on day two.

## If you have already been caught

If money has gone out, the steps are the same everywhere.

1. **Stop at the first transfer.** A second request "to unlock the first" is the same scheme; it ends when you stop paying.
2. **Keep everything.** Chat screenshots, payment details, the platform link, transfer times: the bank or the police will ask.
3. **Report where the money left from.** A reversal is rare, but a report marks the recipient.
4. **Warn the chat.** One message with the platform's name and a screenshot protects dozens.
5. **Do not quit clipping.** Pick one from [the best clipping platforms](/en/earnings/best-clipping-platforms), run the checklist and start with one task.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is clipping legit?

Yes. Pay per view is an ordinary advertising deal: a brand buys reach, a clipper produces it. A scam is a specific platform or person, exposed by the flags above.

### Honest platform, small money. Is that a flag?

No, that is the market: a modest rate and a cap mean the buyer counts their money. The flag is the opposite: big promises, no published terms.

### Is a platform with no reviews safe?

Reviews are easy to buy, terms are hard to fake. Check the mechanics: task numbers, where the budget sits, how views are counted, documents, a name.

### What if I already paid for access?

Pay nothing more, keep the chat and payment details, write to the bank, warn the chat. A refund is unlikely; stopping early matters most.
