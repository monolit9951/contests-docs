## How to read the table

A clipping platform is a marketplace where brands, streamers and creators fund campaigns, and clippers cut the source footage into short clips, post them on their own accounts and are paid per counted view. The "$ per 1,000 views" column is the number every platform advertises: the rate as the platform publishes it. On its own it is the least useful number. What reaches you is the rate after the platform's fee, only on clips that cleared the view threshold, and never above the cap per clip; and only if the platform can pay to your country at all.

The table puts the same nine fields next to twenty-one platforms: the global boards first, then the boards that pay in rupees, rupiah, reais or rubles. A grey "not published" means the platform does not state that figure anywhere on its own pages, which is itself something to know before you clip for it. A superscript number links to the page the figure was read from, and the date on that link is the day we read it. Where a platform pays in its own currency, the dollar figure in brackets is our estimate: cross rates from the European Central Bank's euro reference rates of 23 September 2026, ₹95.74, Rp17,795 and R$5.13 per dollar<!-- source: https://www.ecb.europa.eu/stats/eurofxref/eurofxref-hist-90d.xml 2026-09-24 -->, and for rubles at the Bank of Russia rate of 24 September 2026, 84.40 ₽ per dollar<!-- source: https://www.cbr.ru/scripts/XML_daily.asp?date_req=24/09/2026 2026-09-24 -->.

They are not clipping platforms: five of the six set an audience threshold before they pay, Facebook's program is by invitation, and all six exclude reposted clips of someone else's video. ## Which platform is right for you

If you clip from Russia, Belarus, Ukraine, Kazakhstan or elsewhere in CIS, start with the platforms that state they pay there. Whop's sanctioned-country list blocks Russia, Belarus and six regions of Ukraine (Crimea, Sevastopol, Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia, Kherson); Vyro's unsupported list covers Russia, Belarus, all of Ukraine, Uzbekistan, Armenia, Georgia and Moldova; Ssemble pays its own campaigns only through Stripe to the countries it lists, and none of the four is among them<!-- source: https://www.ssemble.com/clip-rewards 2026-09-24 -->. DareBay has no country list at all: it pays worldwide in USDT on TON or Telegram Stars with no follower requirement, and its community is largest in exactly this region; Prime Oracles pays Russia, Belarus and Kazakhstan in USDT or USDC from $5; Klipni pays in rubles but requires Russian self-employed status. Promote.fun, FindClout, Clipster, Cliptocash and Clipping.io publish no country list, so ask their support in writing before your first clip.

If you clip from the US or the EU and want the largest choice of campaigns, join Whop Content Rewards first: 684 campaigns were live on its Discover board on 24 September 2026<!-- source: https://contentrewards.com/discover 2026-09-24 -->. Then add Clipster, whose public board listed 89 the same day, mostly TikTok music edits, gaming and streamer clips<!-- source: https://www.clipster.gg/discover 2026-09-24 -->, and then Vyro and Clipping.net. Campaign budgets are finite, and a clipper who depends on one platform sits idle when its campaigns drain. Before your first clip check the fee on payout, the threshold and the minimum payout: Whop's terms take 10% of CPM payouts, Vyro pays nothing on a post under 5,000 views, and Vues does not publish a clipper fee rate (its homepage puts its cut on the brand's budget and shows clippers a processing fee on withdrawal<!-- source: https://vues.app/ 2026-09-18 -->, while its referral page illustrates a 10% platform fee on each cash-out<!-- source: https://vues.app/affiliate 2026-09-18 -->).

If you are new and have no audience, prefer platforms with no follower requirement and a printed rate, threshold and cap per campaign, so that you can calculate the payout of a clip before you edit it. Every DareBay task card carries all three, and Clipster's public board shows them for each campaign.

If you want to be paid in your own currency, the regional boards do that: Wondeed, ClipConnect India and ClipGrow pay rupees to UPI; Clippo, Ternak Klip and Konten.com pay rupiah to a local bank account, and Clippo and Konten.com to an e-wallet too; Cut.Pro pays reais over PIX to a key in your own name<!-- source: https://cut.pro/championships-policy 2026-09-24 -->; Klipni pays rubles to Russian self-employed clippers. Each one pays in its own currency to local accounts, so it answers a different question from the global boards: not "who pays the most" but "who pays into the account I already have".

If you want more than a rate per view, look at what a task adds on top. DareBay's offer tasks pay a fixed fee per accepted clip and a share of the orders your promo code brings, and hand you ready-made videos with built-in uniqueization so you post without editing software.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which clipping platforms pay per view in 2026?

This page compares twenty-one. The global boards are Whop Content Rewards, Vyro, DareBay, Promote.fun, Ssemble Clip Rewards, Prime Oracles, Reach.cat, FindClout, Clipster, Clipping.net, Vues, Cliptocash (cliptocash.com) and Clipping.io; the regional ones pay in rupees (Wondeed, ClipConnect India, ClipGrow), in rupiah (Clippo, Ternak Klip, Konten.com), in reais (Cut.Pro) or in rubles (Klipni). Advertised rates run from one cent to $10 per 1,000 views, and the table above lines all of them up on the same nine fields.

### What is the best clipping platform in 2026?

There is no single best platform; it depends on where you live and what you clip. For US and EU clippers who want the widest choice of campaigns, Whop Content Rewards and Clipster have the most supply. DareBay has no country exclusion list, which matters most for clippers in Russia and Belarus, where neither Whop nor Vyro pays, and in Ukraine, which Vyro excludes and Whop closes in six regions. Compare the fee, cap and minimum payout, not only the rate.

### Which clipping platform pays the most per 1,000 views?

The highest advertised rates are not the highest net pay. Whop's clipping campaigns run from $0.01 to $10 per 1,000, with $1 the most common rate (244 of the 684 campaigns live on 24 September 2026)<!-- source: https://contentrewards.com/discover 2026-09-24 -->, but its terms take 10% of CPM payouts; DareBay's open contests pay $1–$10, with nothing taken from contest payouts and 10% taken when you withdraw the balance; Prime Oracles' open rewards reached $8<!-- source: https://www.primeoracles.com/rewards 2026-09-19 -->, with 10% taken at withdrawal<!-- source: https://www.primeoracles.com/terms-agreement 2026-09-19 -->; Clipster's live campaigns go from $0.05 to $4<!-- source: https://www.clipster.gg/discover 2026-09-24 -->; Vyro pays $1–$2 per 1,000 only after 5,000 views per post; Clipping.net quotes $0.20–$0.75+ per 1,000. Sort the table by the "$ per 1,000 views" column, then read the fee, threshold and cap columns for the same row.

### Do I need followers to join a clipping platform?

On most campaign-based platforms, no: you are paid for the views of the clip you post, not for your follower count. Whop, Clipping.net, Vues, Ssemble and DareBay state no follower minimum. Reach.cat and Clipster have no platform-wide minimum but let a campaign set one, and 15 of Clipster's 89 live campaigns asked for 100 to 1,000 followers per profile<!-- source: https://www.clipster.gg/discover 2026-09-24 -->; FindClout admits creators by application and reviews their audience first<!-- source: https://app.findclout.com/ 2026-09-24 -->. The social networks' own programs are a different matter: TikTok's needs 10,000 followers and YouTube's 1,000 subscribers.

### Do clipping platforms charge clippers a fee?

Many do, either as a percentage of the payout or as a withdrawal fee. Whop's terms take 10% of CPM payouts; Klipni takes 5% of each approved payout; Konten.com takes 20% of gross earnings plus Rp10,000 per withdrawal<!-- source: https://konten.com/terms 2026-09-18 -->; Ternak Klip takes 20% of creator payouts<!-- source: https://ternakklip.com/home 2026-09-18 -->; ClipConnect India deducts 10% from the clipper's earnings<!-- source: https://clipconnect.in/ 2026-09-18 -->; FindClout states 0% commission on creator earnings<!-- source: https://app.findclout.com/for-creators 2026-09-24 -->. Vues does not publish a rate and its pages disagree (its homepage puts its cut on the brand's budget, its referral page illustrates a 10% platform fee on each cash-out<!-- source: https://vues.app/affiliate 2026-09-18 -->), Reach.cat's terms name a commission of approximately 10% on all CPM-based payouts<!-- source: https://reach.cat/terms 2026-09-18 --> while its blog says the brand pays the 10% and clippers keep 100%<!-- source: https://reach.cat/blog/best-clipping-platforms/ 2026-09-18 -->, and Clipping.net, Vyro, Clipster and Promote.fun do not publish a clipper fee. DareBay takes 0% on contest payouts and 10% on balance withdrawals: requests start at 10 USDT and the team checks the details before sending.

### Can I clip for several platforms at once?

Yes, and it is the sensible default: campaigns run out of budget, so briefs from two or three sources keep you working. Check each campaign's exclusivity rules before posting the same clip in two places.

### Which clipping platforms pay in Russia and CIS?

DareBay pays worldwide with no country list, so Russia, Belarus, Ukraine, Kazakhstan and the rest of CIS are included, and that is where its community is largest. Prime Oracles serves Russia, Belarus and Kazakhstan in crypto. Klipni pays in rubles to Russian self-employed clippers. Whop excludes Russia, Belarus and six regions of Ukraine, Vyro all three countries, and Ssemble's own campaigns pay only to its Stripe country list, which has none of them; Clipping.net, Vues, Reach.cat, Clipster, Promote.fun, FindClout, Cliptocash and Clipping.io publish no country list.

### Do TikTok and YouTube pay per view too?

Yes, through their own creator programs rather than brand campaigns: TikTok's Creator Rewards Program, YouTube Shorts revenue sharing, Facebook Content Monetization, Instagram bonuses, Snapchat's Monetization Program and X's Original Content Rewards. None of them publishes a rate per 1,000 views, five of the six set an audience threshold and Facebook's is by invitation, and all six exclude reposted clips of someone else's video; who can join each one is in the table above.
