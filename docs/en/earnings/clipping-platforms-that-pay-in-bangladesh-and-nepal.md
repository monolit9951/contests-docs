A clipper in Dhaka or Kathmandu has two questions: does a platform's money reach this country, and does it arrive on a rail the central bank accepts. Here the second question removes more options than the first. Platforms are ranked on [the best clipping platforms](/en/earnings/best-clipping-platforms), rates are on [how much clipping pays](/en/earnings/how-much-clipping-pays), and the neighbouring markets have their own pages on [Pakistan](/en/earnings/clipping-platforms-that-pay-in-pakistan) and [India](/en/earnings/clipping-platforms-that-pay-in-india).

## Payoneer into bKash: the limits bKash itself publishes

bKash publishes this route itself, as a service for freelancers: earnings move from Payoneer into a bKash account "without any paperwork", from BDT 1,000 up to "BDT 2,50,000 in a single transaction", with no daily or monthly cap. The only price is Payoneer's, "3% Conversion Fee + $1 for every transaction", and the government's 2.5% remittance incentive "does not apply to this service" <!-- source: https://www.bkash.com/en/products-services/payoneer 2026-09-18 -->. Payoneer also offers withdrawal to a local bank, which can take as little as one working day <!-- source: https://www.payoneer.com/resources/business/pay-your-contractors-and-remote-employees-in-bangladesh/ 2026-09-18 -->.

Nagad, Wise and Xoom serve a different sender. Nagad's remittance service aims to serve "the money transfer needs of Non-Resident Bangladeshi population" through Western Union and partner transfer operators, Payoneer not among them <!-- source: https://nagad.com.bd/services/?service=remittance 2026-09-18 -->. Wise delivers taka to banks, bKash and Nagad, but "You can't send business transfers to BDT" <!-- source: https://wise.com/help/articles/2932161/guide-to-bdt-transfers 2026-09-18 -->, and it cannot hold money for a resident of Bangladesh <!-- source: https://wise.com/help/articles/2813542/where-do-i-need-to-live-to-hold-money-with-wise 2026-09-18 -->. Xoom pays into bKash for people sending to friends and family <!-- source: https://www.xoom.com/bangladesh/send-money 2026-09-18 -->: money from relatives, not a business payout.

## The freelancer rules Bangladesh Bank restated in July 2026

On 22 July 2026 Bangladesh Bank put its freelancer rules into one circular. A bank may credit a payment on electronic evidence, "emails, contracts, invoices (if available), platform statements", with no Form-C up to USD 20,000. Online payment gateways may carry payments of up to USD 10,000 each, and wallet providers working with a foreign partner may credit "digital wallets or bank accounts of freelancers", the footing of the bKash route. Up to 50% of net earnings may stay in foreign currency for software, data entry and other ICT-related services, and up to 30% for other services <!-- source: https://www.bb.org.bd/mediaroom/circulars/fepd/jul222026fepd-122e.pdf 2026-09-18 -->.

## What Bangladesh Bank says about being paid in USDT

FE Circular No. 24 of 15 September 2022 says the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act, 1947 "does not recognize virtual currencies", that "Any transactions made in/from/to Bangladesh for obtaining virtual assets … are not permitted by Bangladesh Bank", and that "all individuals/entities/institutions operating in Bangladesh shall refrain from dealing in virtual assets, virtual currencies" <!-- source: https://www.bb.org.bd/mediaroom/circulars/fepd/sep152022fepd24e.pdf 2026-09-18 -->.

The export-earnings line is current; the consolidated export circular of 30 July 2026 says: "Retention of export proceeds abroad, in any form (including foreign accounts, assets, or virtual assets), other than permitted notional/merchant accounts under approved arrangements, shall constitute a violation of the Foreign Exchange Regulation Act, 1947" <!-- source: https://www.bb.org.bd/mediaroom/circulars/fepd/jul302026fepd-126e.pdf 2026-09-18 -->. Banks were told in October 2022 to stop helping peer-to-peer crypto trading through customers' accounts <!-- source: https://www.bb.org.bd/mediaroom/circulars/brpd/oct122022brpdl40.pdf 2026-09-18 -->, and on 30 July 2026 the bank warned the public off unauthorised crypto platforms, which it says are not licensed by Bangladesh Bank or the relevant regulator <!-- source: https://www.bb.org.bd/mediaroom/press_release/press/pr14200_20260730.pdf 2026-09-18 -->.

A token-only platform therefore offers a rail Bangladesh Bank says is not permitted; this page quotes the regulator and stops there.

## Tax, and two different 2.5 percent figures

The Income Tax Act, 2023 exempts listed digital-service income of Bangladeshi individuals until 30 June 2027 if "all income, expenditure and investment of the business shall be performed wholly through bank transfer" <!-- source: https://nbr.gov.bd/uploads/acts/3190-SRO-404-NBR-16_October_2025(10455-10741).pdf 2026-09-18 -->, and from 1 July 2026 the Finance Act 2026 widens the listed "IT Freelancing" to freelancing and content creation <!-- source: https://nbr.gov.bd/uploads/acts/Finance_Act_2026.pdf 2026-09-18 -->. Whether clip editing counts is for the National Board of Revenue to say. Of the two 2.5% figures, one is Bangladesh Bank's 2026–27 export incentive for individual freelancers on software and ITES services <!-- source: https://www.bb.org.bd/mediaroom/circulars/fepd/jul052026fepd-117.pdf 2026-09-18 -->; the other, the bonus on remittances from Bangladeshis working abroad, does not apply to Payoneer money arriving in bKash <!-- source: https://www.bkash.com/en/products-services/payoneer 2026-09-18 -->.

## Nepal: a PayPal site that can pay but not be paid

Nepal is on PayPal's country list <!-- source: https://www.paypal.com/us/webapps/mpp/country-worldwide 2026-09-18 -->, but the Nepal site offers "Send payments" and "Pay securely" with no way to request money <!-- source: https://www.paypal.com/np/home 2026-09-18 -->, and Nepal is not among the 93 countries that can receive a PayPal payout <!-- source: https://developer.paypal.com/payouts/supported-features 2026-09-18 -->. PayPal's help centre gives the rule: "Members in some countries and regions may only use PayPal to send payments and not receive payments" <!-- source: https://www.paypal.com/np/cshelp/article/why-does-my-paypal-account-have-only-send-capabilities-help1027 2026-09-18 -->. Wise sends rupees only to bank accounts and "can't send NPR to businesses in Nepal" <!-- source: https://wise.com/help/articles/2932337/guide-to-npr-transfers 2026-09-18 -->. That is how Vyro can leave Nepal off its unsupported list and still have no rail to it: "we only support PayPal or Stripe for withdrawals" <!-- source: https://vyro.com/help/earnings-and-payments/how-can-i-withdraw-my-earnings 2026-09-18 -->.

## Stablecoins included: Nepal Rastra Bank's own wording

Nepal Rastra Bank's notice of 2079/04/30 BS (15 August 2022) says, in our rendering from Nepali, that virtual currency and cryptocurrency, "stablecoins included", has no legal recognition in Nepal and that transactions in it are "illegal"; residents of Nepal and Nepali citizens abroad found using, investing in, owning, transferring, remitting or exchanging it "will face action under the prevailing law" <!-- source: https://www.nrb.org.np/contents/uploads/2022/08/FXMD-Notice-2_207980_Cryptocurrecy.pdf 2026-09-18 -->. A notice of 2079/12/20 BS (3 April 2023) adds NFTs, digital assets and DeFi <!-- source: https://www.nrb.org.np/contents/uploads/2023/04/FXMD-Notice-Cryptocurrency_2079.12.20.pdf 2026-09-18 -->. Both are reprinted in the bank's consolidated foreign exchange circular of April 2026 <!-- source: https://www.nrb.org.np/contents/uploads/2026/04/for-upload-UC.pdf 2026-09-18 -->, and its FAQ says in one line that such trading "is not allowed" <!-- source: https://www.nrb.org.np/2023/08/%e0%a4%a8%e0%a5%87%e0%a4%aa%e0%a4%be%e0%a4%b2%e0%a4%ae%e0%a4%be-bitcoin-%e0%a4%b2%e0%a4%97%e0%a4%be%e0%a4%af%e0%a4%a4%e0%a4%95%e0%a4%be-cryptocurrency-%e0%a4%b9%e0%a4%b0%e0%a5%81%e0%a4%95%e0%a5%8b-2/ 2026-09-18 -->. A 2020 notice adds that receiving money from abroad through informal channels for advertising on Facebook, Twitter or YouTube is illegal <!-- source: https://www.nrb.org.np/contents/uploads/2020/03/Social-Media-and-Online-Pmt..pdf 2026-09-18 -->.

## Khalti's Stripe link, eSewa and a Nepali bank account

Since April 2026 a KYC-verified Khalti user can send a payment request, entering a PAN; the payer abroad passes "a quick, one-time identity verification (liveness + document)" and pays by card, wallet or bank through Stripe <!-- source: https://blog.khalti.com/services-in-khalti/khalti-stripe-international-payments-nepal/ 2026-09-18 -->. Rupees reach the Khalti wallet less a 2% service fee and a 5% advance tax deducted before payout <!-- source: https://blog.khalti.com/offers/frequently-asked-questions-receive-international-payments-directly-in-your-khalti-wallet-via-stripe/ 2026-09-18 -->. It suits a streamer or brand paying you directly.

eSewa receives remittances from Nepalis abroad through partners such as Remitly, Ria and MoneyGram <!-- source: https://blog.esewa.com.np/send-remittance-to-esewa-wallet 2026-09-18 -->. The route Nepal Rastra Bank's circular writes down is a bank account: a payment to a person for services sold abroad is credited against a copy of their PAN registration and an anti-money-laundering self-declaration, and a bank's foreign-currency prepaid "dollar card" must accept earnings from online service exports, with anything above USD 5,000 moved to an account <!-- source: https://www.nrb.org.np/contents/uploads/2026/04/for-upload-UC.pdf 2026-09-18 -->.

When a resident individual not running a business is paid in foreign currency "for uploading audiovisual content on social media", the bank or money-transfer company collects 5% advance tax <!-- source: https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/pdf_upload/%E0%A4%86%E0%A4%AF%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%B0%20%E0%A4%90%E0%A4%A8%2C%20%E0%A5%A8%E0%A5%A6%E0%A5%AB%E0%A5%AE%20(%E0%A4%86%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A5%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%95%20%E0%A4%90%E0%A4%A8%2C%E0%A5%A8%E0%A5%A6%E0%A5%AE%E0%A5%A8%20%E0%A4%B2%E0%A5%87%20%E0%A4%97%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%87%E0%A4%95%E0%A5%8B%20%E0%A4%B8%E0%A4%82%E0%A4%B6%E0%A5%8B%E0%A4%A7%E0%A4%A8%20%E0%A4%B8%E0%A4%B9%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%A4)_7h85rod.pdf 2026-09-18 -->, and from mid-July 2026 the Finance Act 2083 makes that deduction final <!-- source: https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/pdf_upload/%E0%A4%86%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A5%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%95%20%E0%A4%90%E0%A4%A8%2C%20%E0%A5%A8%E0%A5%A6%E0%A5%AE%E0%A5%A9%20(%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%9C%E0%A4%AA%E0%A4%A4%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%BE%20%E0%A4%AA%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%B0%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B6%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%A4%20%E0%A4%AE%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%BF%20%E0%A5%A8%E0%A5%A6%E0%A5%AE%E0%A5%A9%E0%A5%A4%E0%A5%A6%E0%A5%A9%E0%A5%A4%E0%A5%A9%E0%A5%A6)_vzvsbtv.pdf 2026-09-18 --> <!-- source: https://giwmscdnone.gov.np/media/pdf_upload/%E0%A4%9C%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%A8%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%BE%E0%A4%B0%E0%A5%80%E0%A4%AE%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%B2%E0%A4%95%20%E0%A4%AA%E0%A5%81%E0%A4%B8%E0%A5%8D%E0%A4%A4%E0%A4%BF%E0%A4%95%E0%A4%BE_fajpdfq.pdf 2026-09-18 -->. Khalti's 5% matches that rate; whether clipping counts is for the Inland Revenue Department to say.

Read the table by rail. Whop puts both countries on its payout list <!-- source: https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/manage-payouts/set-up-payouts 2026-09-18 --> and neither on its sanctions list <!-- source: https://docs.whop.com/trust-and-safety/trust-safety-overview/sanctioned-countries 2026-09-18 -->, and asks creators outside the United States for a bank account "that accepts the currency of their registered country" <!-- source: https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/manage-payouts/payout-methods 2026-09-18 -->: taka or rupees. DareBay takes 0% from a task payout, but a balance leaves only as USDT on the TON network, 10% deducted from a request of at least 10 USDT; that is the rail neither central bank permits, so it has no lawful payout route here. Clipping.net pays each campaign by PayPal or by USDC or USDT on Ethereum <!-- source: https://clipping.net/docs/clippers/payments 2026-09-18 -->, and Vues leaves the method to the brand <!-- source: https://vues.app/terms 2026-09-18 -->. Reach.cat lists no countries <!-- source: https://reach.cat/become-a-clipper/ 2026-09-18 --> and offers USDT or an IBAN bank transfer <!-- source: https://reach.cat/dashboard/creator/earnings 2026-09-18 -->, with an ID check once payout requests pass $500 <!-- source: https://reach.cat/terms 2026-09-18 -->. [Prime Oracles](/en/earnings/prime-oracles-review) pays in crypto, and its agreement also describes USD to cards and bank accounts through its partner Garna without saying whether that route is open to an ordinary account; it excludes "any jurisdiction in which the use of cryptocurrency services is restricted or prohibited under applicable law" <!-- source: https://www.primeoracles.com/terms-agreement 2026-09-19 -->. Clipping.io publishes nothing on countries or payouts <!-- source: https://www.clipping.io/terms-of-use 2026-09-18 -->. BlueClip, which calls itself "Bangladesh's clipping campaign platform", is not a ninth board: on 19 September 2026 its Whop store held a course, a chat and a forum, and its one post says "payouts run through Whop" <!-- source: https://whop.com/blueclip/ 2026-09-19 -->.

## DareBay in Bangladesh and Nepal: no money route today

DareBay publishes no exclusion list and asks for no followers or application. It takes 0% from a task budget and nothing from what a clip earns; the one charge on a clipper is 10% of a withdrawal, deducted inside a request of at least 10 USDT and checked by a person before it is sent ([DareBay withdrawals](/en/help/darebay-withdrawals), [DareBay fees](/en/help/what-commission)).

The limit is the rail: a balance leaves as USDT on the TON network or as Telegram Stars. Bangladesh Bank says transactions to obtain virtual assets are not permitted and Nepal Rastra Bank calls crypto transactions illegal, so a DareBay balance has no withdrawal route into either country today; a single task can set a card or a bank transfer as its prize if its organizer decides so. Stars are not money: Telegram's terms call them "virtual items" and bar selling or withdrawing a personal Star balance <!-- source: https://telegram.org/tos/stars 2026-09-18 -->. The interface is English, Russian and Ukrainian; there is no Bangla or Nepali version.

The answer here is a fiat rail: Whop, which lists both countries for payouts to a local-currency bank account, or in Bangladesh the Payoneer to bKash route that bKash itself describes.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is PayPal coming to Bangladesh?

That is PayPal's to announce. On 18 September 2026 Bangladesh is on neither PayPal's country list <!-- source: https://www.paypal.com/us/webapps/mpp/country-worldwide 2026-09-18 --> nor its table of countries that can receive a payout <!-- source: https://developer.paypal.com/payouts/supported-features 2026-09-18 -->. PayPal's Xoom delivers into bKash and Bangladeshi banks <!-- source: https://www.xoom.com/bangladesh/send-money 2026-09-18 -->, but that is money a relative sends, not a platform payout.

### How much does Payoneer take on the way to bKash?

bKash publishes the figure: "3% Conversion Fee + $1 for every transaction", with no other charge, on transfers of BDT 1,000 to 250,000 each. Cashing out at a BRAC Bank, City Bank or Q-Cash ATM costs 7 taka per thousand <!-- source: https://www.bkash.com/en/products-services/payoneer 2026-09-18 -->.

### What does Bangladesh Bank say about Binance and USDT?

It names the activity, not an exchange: transactions to obtain virtual assets "are not permitted by Bangladesh Bank", and everyone operating in Bangladesh "shall refrain from dealing in virtual assets" <!-- source: https://www.bb.org.bd/mediaroom/circulars/fepd/sep152022fepd24e.pdf 2026-09-18 -->. Banks were told to stop helping peer-to-peer crypto trading through customers' accounts <!-- source: https://www.bb.org.bd/mediaroom/circulars/brpd/oct122022brpdl40.pdf 2026-09-18 -->. This page does not describe ways around that.

### Nepal is on PayPal's list, so why can't I get paid there?

A country list and a payout list are different documents. PayPal's country picker includes Nepal <!-- source: https://www.paypal.com/us/webapps/mpp/country-worldwide 2026-09-18 -->, its payout table does not <!-- source: https://developer.paypal.com/payouts/supported-features 2026-09-18 -->, and the Nepal site offers sending and paying only <!-- source: https://www.paypal.com/np/home 2026-09-18 -->. A platform paying through PayPal cannot reach a Nepali account.

### Does Nepal Rastra Bank's ban cover stablecoins like USDT?

Yes, by name: its notices call transactions in virtual currency and cryptocurrency, "stablecoins included", illegal, and warn of action for owning, transferring, remitting or exchanging them <!-- source: https://www.nrb.org.np/contents/uploads/2022/08/FXMD-Notice-2_207980_Cryptocurrecy.pdf 2026-09-18 -->. On 18 September 2026 the notice is still pinned among the Important Notices on the bank's home page <!-- source: https://www.nrb.org.np/ 2026-09-18 -->.

### Can a Khalti Stripe link collect clipping earnings?

Only from someone who opens it. The payer completes Khalti's payment request after an identity check; you need a PAN and receive rupees less a 2% fee and 5% advance tax <!-- source: https://blog.khalti.com/offers/frequently-asked-questions-receive-international-payments-directly-in-your-khalti-wallet-via-stripe/ 2026-09-18 -->. A platform paying out through its own rails will not.
