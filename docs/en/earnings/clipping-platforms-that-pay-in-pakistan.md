## Pakistan: remove unavailable routes before comparing rates

Vyro's exclusion list no longer includes Pakistan: on 14 November 2026 it named 28 countries, and Pakistan, which the earlier list carried, was not one of them.<!-- source: https://vyro.com/help/earnings-and-payments/eligibility 2026-11-14 --> Being off the list is not a promise of a payout: what is left to check is in the platform table below. Wondeed has dropped its Indian-residency clause and publishes no country list; its guide lists PayPal and USDC/USDT payouts, which does not establish a Pakistani receiving route.<!-- source: https://www.wondeed.com/policies/terms-of-service 2026-11-14 --><!-- source: https://www.wondeed.com/docs/payouts 2026-11-14 -->

Vues now advertises bank or USDC through Stripe depending on country; calling this crypto does not bypass provider screening. On Clipping.net the campaign chooses PayPal, USDC or USDT on Ethereum: a PayPal campaign does not promise a crypto alternative. A Payoneer account helps only when the paying platform accepts it.<!-- source: https://vues.app/ios 2026-11-14 --><!-- source: https://clipping.net/docs/clippers/payments 2026-10-12 -->

## A PayPal line on the campaign card is where it ends here

Before the rate matters, read the rail. PayPal's own worldwide list of countries and regions carries no Pakistan entry: its Asia section runs from Armenia to Vietnam and steps straight from Nepal to the Philippines <!-- source: https://www.paypal.com/us/webapps/mpp/country-worldwide 2026-09-18 -->. Stripe's page of supported countries does not name Pakistan either, in the main list, the extended network or the preview markets <!-- source: https://stripe.com/global 2026-09-18 -->. Wise closes the third door: Pakistan is absent from its list of places you must live in to hold money there <!-- source: https://wise.com/help/articles/2813542/where-do-i-need-to-live-to-hold-money-with-wise 2026-09-18 -->.

Those three absences decide less of the board than they did in September. Vyro was then closed twice over: it withdrew only through PayPal or Stripe, and it named Pakistan on its unsupported list. Since 2 November 2026 its cash-outs have gone to a Whop wallet, in its own words "Whop replaces Stripe and PayPal" <!-- source: https://vyro.com/changelog/more-ways-to-get-paid-with-whop 2026-11-14 -->, and Pakistan is not among the 28 countries it now lists as not eligible for payouts <!-- source: https://vyro.com/help/earnings-and-payments/eligibility 2026-11-14 -->. Clipping.net stays half open: it supports PayPal, USDC on Ethereum and USDT on Ethereum, but each campaign fixes one method and a PayPal campaign cannot be taken in crypto instead <!-- source: https://clipping.net/docs/clippers/payments 2026-09-18 -->. Under Vues' terms of 5 October 2026 payouts go "through the methods a Brand makes available through the Service, which may include cryptocurrency (such as USDT), PayPal, or Zelle" <!-- source: https://vues.app/terms 2026-11-14 -->; its app page advertises a bank account or a USDC wallet through Stripe, depending on country <!-- source: https://vues.app/ios 2026-11-14 -->.

The wallet route runs through JazzCash, which Payoneer describes as instant and open from as little as $1 <!-- source: https://www.payoneer.com/resources/how-to-use-payoneer/jazzcash-and-payoneer-team-up-to-make-freelance-payments-easy/ 2026-09-18 -->; JazzCash says the same from its side, that the wallet receives international freelance payments and remittances <!-- source: https://www.jazzcash.com.pk/store-money/freelance-digital-account 2026-09-18 -->. Timing and cost are published only in general terms: withdrawals typically reach a local bank within one to three business days, fees vary by withdrawal type and are shown before you confirm, and there is no setup or monthly fee <!-- source: https://www.payoneer.com/withdraw-funds/ 2026-09-18 -->. ## The dollar account the State Bank built for freelance earnings

Money arriving through a bank does not arrive anonymously, and Pakistan has a purpose-built account for it. The State Bank's freelancer framework lets an individual open an Exporters' Special Foreign Currency Account alongside the primary rupee account, "in-person or remotely through digital means", and writes eligibility for "all individuals, resident in Pakistan, engaged in provision of any digital/online services … against which payments are received from outside Pakistan" <!-- source: https://www.sbp.org.pk/circulars/bprd-circular-no-05-of-2023 2026-09-18 -->. Inside it a freelancer may keep "USD 5,000/- per month or 50% of the export proceeds, whichever is higher" instead of converting everything to rupees on arrival <!-- source: https://www.sbp.org.pk/epd/2023/FECL17.htm 2026-09-18 -->.

Two 2026 changes matter for payouts the size of a clipping campaign. Form "R" is no longer required for every individual export transaction, replaced by a one-time declaration, and "a maximum turnaround time of one working day" now applies to processing inward export receipts <!-- source: https://archive.sbp.org.pk/press/2026/Pr-06-Apr-2026.pdf 2026-09-18 -->; the reporting threshold for Form "R" and the inward remittance voucher was revised to USD 25,000 <!-- source: https://archive.sbp.org.pk/epd/2026/FECL7.htm 2026-09-18 -->. A clipping payout sits far below that line, so the paperwork you meet is the account opening, not the transfer.

## USDT in Pakistan: what is published, and what nobody has published

Read this part slowly, because the honest answer is not one word. The 2018 starting point is still published on the State Bank's site: banks, microfinance banks and payment service providers were told "to refrain from processing, using, trading, holding, transferring value, promoting and investing in Virtual Currencies/Tokens" <!-- source: https://www.sbp.org.pk/bprd/2018/C3.htm 2026-09-18 -->. The State Bank itself later narrowed what that meant, clarifying that in 2018 it "advised its regulated entities … to avoid dealing in Virtual Assets (VAs) due to the absence of any legal and regulatory framework for the VAs; not because it was declared illegal in the country" <!-- source: https://archive.sbp.org.pk/press/2025/Pr-30-May-2025.pdf 2026-09-18 -->.

Then a framework arrived. A circular dated 14 April 2026 states that "the Virtual Assets Act, 2026 has been enacted", that PVARA is the licensing and supervisory authority, and that it was decided "to replace the BPRD Circular No. 03 of 2018 dated April 06, 2018 … with immediate effect", letting regulated banks open accounts for providers PVARA has licensed while those banks themselves "shall not invest, trade or hold virtual assets using their own funds or customer deposits" <!-- source: https://archive.sbp.org.pk/bprd/2026/CL10.htm 2026-09-18 -->. Two caveats belong beside that sentence. The text above is readable on the State Bank's archive host, while the same document's page on the main site is unreachable and absent from that site's 2026 circular index, and the 2018 circular is still published there. And what gets licensed is the intermediary business: PVARA states that "all Virtual Asset Service Providers must obtain a formal license before offering services in Pakistan", across eleven service categories from exchange to custody to transfer and settlement <!-- source: https://www.pvara.gov.pk/licensing 2026-09-18 -->.

What neither body publishes is a rule about you: nothing in the circular or on PVARA's pages sets out what a private individual may hold, receive or spend. So treat a token-only rail as something to verify rather than assume, and read the current position on both regulators' own sites before you rely on it <!-- source: https://pvara.gov.pk/ 2026-09-18 -->. ## DareBay in Pakistan, and when another platform fits better

DareBay keeps no country list, so no account is turned away for being in Pakistan; only people on sanctions lists are barred <!-- source: https://darebay.com/en/legal/terms 2026-09-24 -->. No follower count, portfolio or application stands between you and a task. Its tasks pay $1.00 to $10.00 per 1,000 counted views, set by each task, with a cap of up to $500 on a single clip, and the view threshold is printed on the card before you start; the calculation and the crediting to your balance are automatic. Nothing is taken from a task budget or from what a clip earns; the one charge a clipper meets is 10% (default) on a withdrawal, deducted inside a request of at least 10 USDT, with the amount to receive shown before you confirm, and the platform does the rest — see [DareBay withdrawals](/en/help/darebay-withdrawals) and [what fees DareBay charges](/en/help/what-commission).

The balance leaves as USDT on TON, or you receive the payout in Telegram Stars. Stars are not a second way to get money, since Telegram's own terms call them virtual items and say you "may not sell, withdraw, transfer or use Stars in your personal balance for any other purpose than those explicitly outlined in these Terms" <!-- source: https://telegram.org/tos/stars 2026-09-18 -->. As the USDT section above shows, check the local rules before you cash out USDT. The interface is in English, Russian, Ukrainian, Polish and Spanish.

If you would rather be paid into a bank or a mobile wallet, Whop Content Rewards says it does that for Pakistan, with the options chosen by country rather than by you <!-- source: https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/manage-payouts/set-up-payouts 2026-11-14 -->; our [Whop Content Rewards review](/en/earnings/whop-content-rewards-review) covers how its campaigns run. MetaClip, the Roblox board above, says it pays into a Pakistani bank account <!-- source: https://metaclip.app/payouts 2026-11-14 -->, and Tsunami is the rupee-priced board, with no live campaign on 14 November 2026 <!-- source: https://www.sunaami.com/ 2026-11-14 -->. If you would rather stay on a crypto wallet with no PayPal in the chain and on a network other than TON, Prime Oracles pays USDT on Tron, BNB Smart Chain or Solana and USDC on Ethereum or Solana <!-- source: https://www.primeoracles.com/terms-agreement 2026-11-14 -->, under the clause quoted in the table above that shuts out any jurisdiction where crypto services are restricted or prohibited; a Clipping.net campaign can pay USDC or USDT on Ethereum, but the campaign fixes the method, not you <!-- source: https://clipping.net/docs/clippers/payments 2026-10-12 -->. ### Is USDT legal in Pakistan?

The published position is narrower than a yes or a no. ### Can I receive a clipping payout through Raast?

No route is described for that. The State Bank presents Raast as Pakistan's free instant payment system, through which bank customers send and receive funds in their accounts using their bank's app, internet banking or a branch <!-- source: https://archive.sbp.org.pk/dfs/Raast-P2P.html 2026-09-18 -->, and its published cross-border work is a corridor with the Arab region rather than a general inbound channel. A foreign platform reaches you through Payoneer or its own partner; Raast moves the money once it is already inside the country.
