## What $100 looks like in views

The first hundred is not one lucky clip but the sum of counted views across every submission that cleared the threshold. The platform runs one formula: views / 1,000 × rate, never above the cap per clip. Each task sets its rate between $1 and $10 per 1,000 views, so a hundred is 100,000 counted views at $1 or 10,000 at $10.

The threshold is set by the task, 1,000 views by default, and it sits on each clip separately. With that default, a clip at 800 views earns nothing; a clip at 1,500 is paid in full, because the threshold opens the count and is not subtracted from it. A hypothetical clip at 8,000 views at $1 brings 8,000 / 1,000 × $1.00 = **$8.00**. The cap per clip, up to $500 where a task sets one, is no obstacle in a first month: at $1 only a clip at half a million views reaches it.

Twelve clips averaging 8,000–9,000 views close the hundred at $1, two at $10; that average is a hypothesis you learn after ten submissions. Put your own numbers into the [clipping earnings calculator](/en/earnings/clipping-earnings-calculator).

## The 30-day plan, week by week

The table is a schedule, not a forecast; the last column is a hypothetical scenario of clips past the threshold. Some beat it in week two, others reach the hundred in week six. The point is a clip count that grows every week.

| Week | What you do | Clips | Counted views, hypothesis |
|---|---|---:|---:|
| 1 | Sign up, two or three tasks on a topic you know, first clip through to a submitted link | 3–4 | 0–10,000 |
| 2 | See which clips cleared the threshold, repeat their format, add a second task | 5–6 | 5,000–20,000 |
| 3 | Batch: two days of editing, five days of posting, first "Claim now" | 7–8 | 15,000–35,000 |
| 4 | Same pace plus a task with ready-made videos, first withdrawal request | 8–10 | 20,000–45,000 |

At the top of the range that is about 110,000 counted views: 110,000 / 1,000 × $1.00 = **$110.00**. At the bottom, 40,000 / 1,000 × $1.00 = **$40.00**, and the hundred moves into month two. Either way you end the month knowing your average reach.

## Which tasks to take first

Three numbers on the card, and in the first month they are worth reading in an unusual order.

- **Threshold.** Set per task, with a system default of 1,000 views. The lower it is, the more of your first clips make it into the count.
- **Rate.** The spread from $1 to $10 per 1,000 views is a factor of ten, the same as the gap between 3,000 and 30,000 views. A topic you watch yourself holds the viewer better than a stranger's niche at a higher rate.
- **Cap.** Set per task, up to $500 per clip, and a task may set none. It does not limit a first month: at $1 only a half-million-view clip would reach it.
- **Ready-made setups, no editing.** On offer tasks the platform hands out finished videos with built-in uniqueization: every clipper gets their own copy, you post it and send the link, and the task can add a fixed fee per accepted clip and a share of sales on top.

The rate next to the threshold and the cap is covered in [how much clipping pays](/en/earnings/how-much-clipping-pays); fees and payout countries across platforms are in [the best clipping platforms](/en/earnings/best-clipping-platforms).

## First-month mistakes

1. **Waiting for one viral clip.** The hundred is built from average clips: three at 8,000 views beat a week of waiting.
2. **Posting before reading the card.** The wrong site, a missing hashtag, a private profile or someone else's footage mean a rejection, and a rejected clip is not paid. The reasons are in [why clips get rejected](/en/earnings/why-clips-get-rejected).
3. **Deleting or reposting a clip that "did not take off".** A deleted clip stops giving a counter; a repost of the same clip is a duplicate, not a second submission.
4. **Buying views to reach the threshold.** A clip with suspicious growth goes on hold, inflated views are filtered out, and the money spent is gone.
5. **Counting money by the site's counter.** Views sync several times a day and fresh accruals mature for 24 hours, so the cabinet trails TikTok by hours.
6. **Quitting after week one.** The first week is calibration: it shows which topics and formats work for you.

If clips from a fresh account stall at a few hundred views, the account is usually the reason, not the edit: see [TikTok shadowban and account warm-up](/en/earnings/account-warm-up-and-shadowban).

## Frequently asked questions

### How many clips go into the first $100?

As many as it takes to reach 100,000 counted views at $1 or 10,000 at $10: twenty clips at an average of 5,000 views, ten at 10,000, and a single clip at 100,000 closes the hundred on its own. Arithmetic, not a forecast.

### What if my first clips fall short of the threshold?

Do not redo or delete them: a clip has until the task is finalised to reach the threshold, and past it the whole counter is paid. Meanwhile look for cards with a lower threshold and study the first three seconds of clips that take off.

### Can I take the first $100 out in one request?

Yes, once the amount is on your balance: credited when the task ends, or earlier with "Claim now" where the task shows it. A request for 100 USDT pays out 90 USDT after the 10% withdrawal fee, which is deducted inside the request and shown before you confirm. The minimum for one request is 10 USDT, so the hundred can also go out in parts.

### Should a beginner start with offer tasks and ready-made videos?

For week one it is a convenient entry: a finished video with built-in uniqueization, nothing to edit, and the same view counter to reach the threshold. Reading the card is still mandatory: site, hashtag, link with your code and dates are written there.
