## Eligibility before the headline rate

Registration, joining a campaign and receiving a payout are three separate checks. Confirm your country, account type, required documents and an accessible payout route before editing. A rate on a homepage cannot answer these questions.

## Nine questions to save with the brief

1. Can you join this campaign and receive its payout?
2. Where is its exact rate published, and can it change for submitted work?
3. Does the threshold apply per clip, per campaign or to earnings?
4. Is the cap a currency amount, a view limit or both?
5. Which fee applies at earning and which applies at withdrawal?
6. Which approval, earning window and hold precede settlement?
7. Can you use the specified payout currency and network?
8. Are followers, selection, ownership checks or identity documents required?
9. Does the platform hold the funds or does the organizer pay directly?

Save the current terms and the card. Missing conditions should be resolved before a batch, rather than guessed from an old payout screenshot.

## Price two offers on the same example clip

For 12,000 eligible views, an illustrative $2 rate per 1,000 views and $20 cap turn an uncapped $24 into $20. Another example at $1.50 with no cap gives $18. If the first offer also deducts a modeled 10% payout fee, it gives the same $18 before separate withdrawal costs. Compare the whole calculation; these inputs do not describe available campaigns.

## Test the complete route with a small batch

Follow a small batch through submission, decision and payment before expanding output. Record differences between the published terms and the observed process. Clear source permissions, settlement and a reachable support contact make repeat work easier to assess.
