## Question 1. Can the platform pay to my country?

The fastest way to waste time is to cut ten clips for a platform that cannot pay you. Whop keeps a sanctioned-country list with Russia, Belarus and six regions of Ukraine (Crimea, Sevastopol, Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia, Kherson); Vyro keeps an unsupported list with Russia, Belarus, Ukraine, Uzbekistan, Armenia, Georgia and Moldova. DareBay keeps no country list at all: only people on sanctions lists are barred<!-- source: https://darebay.com/en/legal/terms 2026-09-24 -->, so clippers from Russia, Belarus, Ukraine, Kazakhstan and the rest of CIS are paid the same way as everyone else<!-- source: https://darebay.com/en/legal/terms 2026-09-24 -->. [Prime Oracles](/en/earnings/prime-oracles-review) names ten CIS states in the tax clause of its terms, Ukraine not among them; its AML policy applies enhanced checks to residents of Russia and Belarus, and it bars residents and nationals of the EU, the UK and the US<!-- source: https://www.primeoracles.com/terms-agreement 2026-09-24 -->. Klipni is Russia-only in practice: its payouts require Russian self-employed or sole-trader tax status<!-- source: https://klipni.com/legal/terms 2026-09-24 -->. Clipping.net, Vues, Reach.cat and Clipping.io do not publish which countries they pay: that is not a "no", but it is a question for support before your first clip.

## Question 2. What is the rate per 1,000 views, and who sets it?

Almost everywhere the campaign owner sets the rate and the platform only prints it on the card. What matters is that the rate is printed before you submit and does not change after the start: that is how DareBay works, where tasks pay $1–$10 per 1,000 views, set by each task<!-- source: https://darebay.com/en/earnings/how-much-clipping-pays 2026-09-24 -->. Klipni prints the rate on the card too, but its advertiser terms let a brand change a running campaign's pay conditions through the operator, and the change applies only to clips sent in after it<!-- source: https://klipni.com/legal/advertiser 2026-09-24 -->. Vues shows rates only inside the app, Reach.cat gives different ranges on different pages, Clipping.io publishes no examples, and every Prime Oracles reward card prints its rate. Across the rewards open on 24 September 2026 those rates ran from $0.05 to $8.00 per 1,000 views: clip cards printed $0.20–$1.00 per 1,000 views, and the $8 was for long YouTube reviews of Prime Oracles itself<!-- source: https://www.primeoracles.com/rewards 2026-09-24 -->.

## Question 3. What is the view threshold?

The threshold is the number of views below which a clip pays nothing at all. Vyro sets 5,000 views per post<!-- source: https://vyro.com/help/earnings-and-payments/how-many-views-do-i-need-before-i-can-start-earning 2026-09-24 -->. Clipping.net sets 1,000 per post; its terms say a campaign typically needs 25,000 in total, and its campaign pages most often list a minimum of 100,000<!-- source: https://clipping.net/policies/clipper-terms-and-conditions 2026-09-24 -->. On DareBay each task sets its own threshold and prints it on the card; Whop has no platform-wide threshold, but the brand may set a minimum a clip must earn before it is paid<!-- source: https://contentrewards.com/terms 2026-09-24 -->. A 5,000-view threshold at $1 per 1,000 views means a clip that stops at 4,999 views earns nothing rather than almost $5.

## Question 4. What is the cap per clip?

The cap limits what one clip can earn, and it is what cuts your income on a viral clip. On Vyro a clip stops earning after 1 million views<!-- source: https://vyro.com/help/earnings-and-payments/whats-the-most-i-can-earn-per-clip 2026-09-24 -->; Whop's help centre calls $200 the usual cap per clip, while the campaign pages we checked on 24 September 2026 ran from $80 to $1,500 per clip<!-- source: https://help.contentrewards.com/en/articles/15319800-which-pricing-model-should-i-use 2026-09-24 -->. A DareBay contest can set a cap on one submission, up to $500 per clip<!-- source: https://darebay.com/en/earnings/how-much-clipping-pays 2026-09-24 -->. A cap is the price of a budget that lasts for everyone; what matters is that it is written in advance.

## Question 5. What is the fee on the payout?

DareBay takes 0% on contest payouts and 10% on balance withdrawals: requests start at 10 USDT, the form shows the amount to receive before you confirm, and the team checks the details before sending. Whop's terms take 10% of every CPM payout (its help centre says 7% may still apply to campaigns from before its relaunch), and Whop's own withdrawal fee comes on top<!-- source: https://contentrewards.com/terms 2026-09-24 -->. Klipni takes 5%. Vues does not publish a rate: its homepage says its cut comes from the brand, its referral page illustrates a 10% fee on each cash-out<!-- source: https://vues.app/affiliate 2026-09-24 -->; Reach.cat bills brands 10% on top of the budget<!-- source: https://reach.cat/blog/reach-cat-pricing-brands-2026/ 2026-09-24 -->, while its terms cite about 10% on all CPM-based payouts<!-- source: https://reach.cat/terms 2026-09-24 -->. Clipping.net and Vyro publish no clipper fee, which is a question for support.

## Question 6. What is the minimum payout, and how fast is it?

At Whop, Content Rewards' pricing page sets no minimum for moving earnings to your Whop balance, while its help centre sets a $1 minimum<!-- source: https://contentrewards.com/pricing/creators 2026-09-24 -->, and Whop's own withdrawal guide names a $10 minimum "if applicable"<!-- source: https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/manage-payouts/set-up-payouts 2026-09-24 -->; a clip earns for 7 days after approval plus a 3-day hold<!-- source: https://contentrewards.com/terms 2026-09-24 -->. Vyro has a $10 minimum and one cash-out every 7 days<!-- source: https://vyro.com/help/earnings-and-payments/how-can-i-withdraw-my-earnings 2026-09-24 -->, and its earnings typically become withdrawable 7–10 days after the campaign ends<!-- source: https://vyro.com/help/earnings-and-payments/when-will-i-receive-my-earnings 2026-09-24 -->. Prime Oracles' agreement sets a minimum of $5 to $12 by network<!-- source: https://www.primeoracles.com/terms-agreement 2026-09-24 -->. Klipni has no minimum<!-- source: https://klipni.com/creators 2026-09-24 --> and, after a hold each campaign sets, pays within 5 business days once the bank accepts the payout<!-- source: https://klipni.com/legal/terms 2026-09-24 -->. On DareBay, where a task shows Claim now, you can lock in what a clip has earned before the task ends, and that clip then stops earning<!-- source: https://darebay.com/en/help/darebay-withdrawals 2026-09-24 -->; otherwise a wallet-backed task credits it after the task ends<!-- source: https://darebay.com/en/help/darebay-withdrawals 2026-09-24 -->. Withdrawal requests start at 10 USDT, and the team checks and sends each one<!-- source: https://darebay.com/en/help/darebay-withdrawals 2026-09-24 -->. Clipping.net and Vues name no minimum.

## Question 7. How do they pay?

Check that you can actually receive the rail. DareBay pays USDT on TON, or you can receive the payout in Telegram Stars<!-- source: https://darebay.com/en/help/darebay-withdrawals 2026-09-24 -->. Whop pays to bank, mobile wallets and crypto; Content Rewards' FAQ also lists PayPal, though Whop's docs tie PayPal payouts to PayPal purchases<!-- source: https://docs.whop.com/manage-your-business/manage-payouts/payout-methods 2026-09-24 -->. Vyro pays only by PayPal or Stripe; Clipping.net by PayPal or USDC/USDT on Ethereum; Vues by USDT, Solana, BTC, PayPal or bank per its homepage<!-- source: https://vues.app/ 2026-09-24 -->. Reach.cat pays in USDT, with bank transfer "very soon" per its creator FAQ, though its pages disagree on bank transfer and PayPal<!-- source: https://reach.cat/creator 2026-09-24 -->; Prime Oracles in USDT, USDC or TON<!-- source: https://www.primeoracles.com/terms-agreement 2026-09-24 -->; Klipni in rubles to the bank account of a Russian self-employed person or sole trader<!-- source: https://klipni.com/legal/terms 2026-09-24 -->.

If PayPal and Stripe are not available where you live, start from the rail rather than the shortlist: [clipping platforms that pay without PayPal or Stripe](/en/earnings/clipping-platforms-that-pay-without-paypal-or-stripe) sets out which of these platforms send a stablecoin, which send Telegram Stars, and which end at a bank account.

## Question 8. Do I need followers, an application or documents?

Followers are required almost nowhere: DareBay, Clipping.net, Vues and Reach.cat say so directly, and so does Vyro's About page<!-- source: https://vyro.com/about 2026-09-24 -->; Whop publishes no follower minimum<!-- source: https://contentrewards.com/terms 2026-09-24 -->, and Klipni sets none platform-wide, though a campaign can set one<!-- source: https://klipni.com/legal/terms 2026-09-24 -->. An application is needed on some Whop and Vues campaigns. Identity verification is required at Whop before payouts and at Reach.cat, under its terms, once total payout requests pass $500<!-- source: https://reach.cat/terms 2026-09-24 -->. Klipni requires Russian self-employed or sole-trader tax status<!-- source: https://klipni.com/legal/terms 2026-09-24 -->. DareBay asks for no followers, no application and no tax status to take a brief; its terms say identity verification applies where the law requires it<!-- source: https://darebay.com/en/legal/terms 2026-09-24 -->.

## Question 9. Where does the budget sit while I clip?

If the budget sits with the platform before clips are posted, the promise to pay does not depend on the client's mood. DareBay locks the budget in wallet-backed contests and labels the manual-payout contests where the organizer pays, Whop holds the brand's budget against the campaign, and Prime Oracles freezes the reward balance. Klipni takes prepayment unless it agrees otherwise in writing, but its advertiser terms call the balance an accounting figure, not escrow<!-- source: https://klipni.com/legal/advertiser 2026-09-24 -->. Vues states that it does not custody funds; Clipping.net does not say, and Clipping.io describes no upfront budget: its homepage tells brands they are charged only once their content reaches the views they need<!-- source: https://www.clipping.io/ 2026-09-24 -->.

## Frequently asked questions

### Which question should I start with?

Country. If a platform cannot pay you, the other eight questions do not matter. Then rate, threshold and cap, because they decide what a clip pays, and only then the fee and the payout rails.

### What if a platform does not publish something?

Ask support before your first clip and keep the answer. A missing number is also information: a platform that does not publish its fee or which countries it pays keeps the right to change them.

### Can I calculate a payout in advance?

Yes, when the rate, threshold and cap are known: payout = rate × views ÷ 1,000, nothing below the threshold and nothing above the cap. Every DareBay task card shows the rate, the threshold and any cap before you submit.

### Should I sign up on several platforms?

Yes. Campaigns run out on every platform, and two or three sources of briefs keep you working. Check a campaign's exclusivity rules before posting the same clip twice.

### Which platform is best for a beginner with no followers?

Start with DareBay, which publishes this page: it asks for no followers or application, so a brand-new account can take a task<!-- source: https://darebay.com/en/earnings/how-much-clipping-pays 2026-09-24 -->, and it keeps no country list, so clippers in Russia, Belarus and Ukraine are paid the same way as everyone else<!-- source: https://darebay.com/en/legal/terms 2026-09-24 -->. On any platform, look for no follower minimum and a rate, threshold and cap printed on the campaign, so the first clip can be calculated before it is cut.
