## The company behind Reach.cat

The terms open with the operator: "Reach.cat is operated by REACH MARKETING NETWORK - FZCO" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/terms 2026-09-18 -->, and the privacy policy gives its address as Building A1, Dubai Digital Park, Dubai Silicon Oasis <!-- source: https://reach.cat/privacy-policy 2026-09-18 -->. The law is that of the United Arab Emirates, with disputes handled "via binding arbitration or through courts in Dubai". At checkout a brand buys from a different company: orders are "sold by Inflowpay SAS, acting as the authorized reseller and Merchant of Record", incorporated in France <!-- source: https://reach.cat/terms 2026-09-18 -->.

The money runs one way. A brand funds its budget plus a 10% platform fee before launch, so a $10,000 budget costs $11,000: "$10,000 to clippers, $1,000 to Reach.cat" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/blog/reach-cat-pricing-brands-2026/ 2026-09-18 -->. The creator terms add "Reach.cat processes payouts internally" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/user-agreement 2026-09-18 -->. The party that pays you is the platform, not the brand; on [Vues](/en/earnings/vues-review) it is the other way round.

Reach.cat "acts strictly as a technical facilitator" and does "not vet, guarantee, or endorse" any campaign or brand; the terms even warn that the platform "may contain offers or content that are illegal, unethical, or banned in certain countries" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/terms 2026-09-18 -->. Checking what you promote is your job.

## What the terms say that the landing pages do not

The clipper landing page answers the usual worries in one line: "No KYC. No ID check. No bank account needed." <!-- source: https://reach.cat/become-a-clipper/ 2026-09-18 --> Another landing page promises "no selfie checks" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/new-whop-alternative/ 2026-09-18 -->, and the creator page says you can "get paid without revealing your real identity" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/creator 2026-09-18 -->.

The creator terms, updated on 13 September 2026, set the limit: once your "cumulative payout requests exceed $500 USD", identity verification "is required before further withdrawals can be processed", through the partner iDenfy, and it "may require submission of a valid government-issued identification document" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/user-agreement 2026-09-18 -->. The main terms add that accounts flagged by fraud checks may need the same verification before joining public campaigns <!-- source: https://reach.cat/terms 2026-09-18 -->. So the check is deferred, not absent.

The same gap runs through four more topics:

- **The payout floor.** Two landing pages advertise cash-outs without any floor. The creator page says "Payouts are processed weekly, once your earnings reach the minimum threshold" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/creator 2026-09-18 -->, and the app's payout screen shows $5 for USDT and $10 for a bank payout (read 2026-09-18) <!-- source: https://reach.cat/dashboard/creator/earnings 2026-09-18 -->.
- **The coin.** The landing page says "USDT or SOL" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/become-a-clipper/ 2026-09-18 -->; a blog post says clippers are paid in "cryptocurrency (USDC)" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/blog/whop-clipping-vs-reach-cat/ 2026-09-18 -->; the payout screen says "Please note that only USDT will be sent."
- **Bank and PayPal.** "No bank account needed" sits beside a payout screen that takes an IBAN. The agency page lists "crypto, bank transfer, PayPal" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/clipping-agencies/ 2026-09-18 -->, while the blog says "Whop supports PayPal; Reach.cat does not" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/blog/best-clipping-platforms/ 2026-09-18 -->, and the payout screen offers no PayPal at all.
- **The rate.** The clipper page promises "$1-3 CPM from day one" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/become-a-clipper/ 2026-09-18 -->, the blog quotes $1–$6 <!-- source: https://reach.cat/blog/best-clipping-platforms/ 2026-09-18 -->, and the brand page says "Most brands see CPMs between $5 and $18" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/ 2026-09-18 -->.

The fee is disputed within the blog itself: one post says "Clippers keep 100% of their CPM earnings" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/blog/best-clipping-platforms/ 2026-09-18 -->, another shows its author "Kept $280.80 after 10% fee" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/blog/tested-every-clipping-platform-2026/ 2026-09-18 -->. The terms speak of "approximately 10% on all CPM-based payouts" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/terms 2026-09-18 -->, and the payout screen shows a $0 fee on USDT and $4 on a bank payout.

## Payout path: clearing, request, claim email

The app explains its sequence in a "How payout works" panel (read 2026-09-18) <!-- source: https://reach.cat/dashboard/creator/earnings 2026-09-18 -->:

1. You submit a clip, and "The campaign manager has up to 3 days to approve it. If no action is taken, the content is automatically approved."
2. An approved clip earns "on a CPM basis, taken from the total campaign budget".
3. A "3-day clearing period" follows: "Earnings are temporarily on hold for 3 days. Our internal team reviews view activity to detect fraud or suspicious behavior."
4. "After the clearing period, you can request your payout at any time."
5. "Funds are sent within 3–5 business days after the request."

The transfer goes through Payouts.com: "When your payout is approved, you will receive an email with instructions to claim your payment", and with a wrong address or network "the funds will be lost and no replacement transfer will be made". The creator terms put approved payouts at "typically" 3–5 business days after final validation and add "Reach.cat does not guarantee exact payment timing" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/user-agreement 2026-09-18 -->. A landing page says "Get paid in hours" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/new-whop-alternative/ 2026-09-18 -->; plan around the terms.

## Rules that can take back money you earned

- **The 60-day rule.** A submitted post "must remain published, public and accessible at the URL you submitted for the whole duration of the campaign and for at least sixty (60) days after the campaign ends". If it disappears early, "unpaid earnings are cancelled, and amounts already credited or paid out may be reversed and deducted from your balance or from your future payouts". It applies to posts submitted from 13 September 2026 <!-- source: https://reach.cat/user-agreement 2026-09-18 -->.
- **New accounts wait.** "accounts created less than thirty (30) days ago may not join a public campaign during the first seven (7) days following its launch" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/terms 2026-09-18 -->.
- **Fraud findings are final.** Suspected botting means termination and "Loss of all funds, including pending earnings"; "Reach.cat's internal assessment is final" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/user-agreement 2026-09-18 -->.
- **No paid boosts.** You may not "Boost, sponsor, whitelist, dark-post, or otherwise promote content through paid advertising", and on termination "Pending or unpaid earnings may be forfeited" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/user-agreement 2026-09-18 -->.
- **One account per person.** Multiple accounts can lead to suspension of all of them <!-- source: https://reach.cat/terms 2026-09-18 -->.

Disputes start with "good-faith informal resolution" and then proceed individually, class and collective actions waived <!-- source: https://reach.cat/user-agreement 2026-09-18 -->.

## Reasons to choose Reach.cat over DareBay

For comparison, DareBay's side of the ledger: a task budget carries no fee, and withdrawing a balance costs 10% of the request, deducted inside it, with requests from 10 USDT. The money leaves in USDT on TON or as Telegram Stars, virtual items that Telegram's terms say you "may not sell, withdraw, transfer" outside its own uses <!-- source: https://telegram.org/tos/stars 2026-09-18 -->. Every request is checked by a person and no settlement time is promised: [the withdrawal rules](/en/help/darebay-withdrawals), [the fee list](/en/help/what-commission).

Reach.cat is the better fit when:

- **Your wallet is not on TON, or you want a bank.** Its payout screen offers USDT on Ethereum, BNB Smart Chain or Solana, or a bank transfer by IBAN; DareBay's withdrawal form offers USDT on TON or Stars.
- **You want a stated payout window.** A 3-day hold, then 3–5 business days after the request, per the app and the creator terms; DareBay promises no settlement time.
- **You cash out small amounts.** The payout screen shows $5 for USDT; a DareBay request starts at 10 USDT.
- **You want the whole USDT payout.** The payout screen shows no deduction on USDT, where DareBay takes 10% inside the request; the terms, though, cite "approximately 10%" on CPM payouts.
- **You clip in French.** The site has French and Catalan versions <!-- source: https://reach.cat/fr 2026-09-18 -->, and the weekly recap lists French-language campaigns such as "Le Code des Grands - la newsletter qui décrypte les milliardaires" <!-- source: https://t.me/reachdotcat/343 2026-09-18 -->.

The trade is the rule set: an ID check past $500, the 60-day rule and final fraud rulings on one side; on DareBay, no country list and, in wallet-backed tasks, a budget locked on the platform before the start. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is Reach.cat legit?

It names its operator in Dubai, publishes terms under UAE law and pays clippers from budgets brands prepay. The caution is its own inconsistency: its landing pages and terms disagree on ID checks, minimums and the coin it pays in.

### Does Reach.cat ask for ID?

Not at sign-up. Its terms effective 13 September 2026 require identity verification through iDenfy once your cumulative payout requests pass $500, and can require it earlier for an account its fraud checks flag.

### How much do you need before a Reach.cat payout?

The app's payout screen shows $5 for USDT and $10 for a bank payout (read 2026-09-18). Two landing pages advertise no floor, and the creator page mentions a minimum threshold without a number.

### Which USDT networks does Reach.cat support?

Ethereum (ERC20), BNB Smart Chain (BEP20) and Solana, per the app's payout screen; only USDT is sent, and a wrong network loses the funds.

### How many days until a Reach.cat payout arrives?

Count the steps: up to 3 days for approval, a 3-day hold, then 3–5 business days after you request. The terms promise no exact timing, and a pending KYC check past $500 holds requests.

### Does Reach.cat take a cut from clippers?

Its pricing puts 10% on top of the brand's budget, yet one blog post deducts 10% from a clipper and the terms cite about 10% on CPM payouts. The payout screen shows $0 on USDT and $4 on a bank payout.

### How long must a Reach.cat post stay online?

For the whole campaign and 60 days after it, public and at the same URL, for posts submitted from 13 September 2026; otherwise earnings can be cancelled or reversed.
