## Reach.cat is closed: what its notice says and what it leaves out

Reach.cat is closed. On 14 November 2026 every page of its app opened under a notice headed "ReachCat has closed its doors". Its text: "The platform permanently closed on November 1, 2026." and "Thank you to every creator and business who trusted us. Building ReachCat with you has been a privilege." <!-- source: https://reach.cat/ 2026-11-14 --> The same notice covered the creator page, both sets of terms, the privacy policy and the sign-in page <!-- source: https://reach.cat/creator 2026-11-14 -->. The sign-up and dashboard addresses led back to the home page under it, so a new account could not be opened. The French version of the site said the same: "La plateforme a définitivement fermé le 1 novembre 2026." <!-- source: https://reach.cat/fr 2026-11-14 -->

The notice has one button, "Contact us", which opens an email to the company, and gives no other instruction <!-- source: https://reach.cat/ 2026-11-14 -->. It does not say:

- what happens to balances that clippers had earned and not yet withdrawn;
- whether payout requests are still processed;
- what happens to budgets that brands had prepaid;
- whether a post must still stay online for 60 days after its campaign, as the creator terms required;
- why the platform closed.

The documents under the notice had not changed since September: the terms still carried "Effective Date: 13/09/2026" and the creator terms "Last Updated: September 13th, 2026", and neither has a clause about the platform closing <!-- source: https://reach.cat/terms 2026-11-14 --> <!-- source: https://reach.cat/user-agreement 2026-11-14 -->. So nothing on its pages says what the closure does to an unpaid balance: not that it is lost, and not that it will be paid. This page does not guess either.

Two of its own channels were still advertising campaigns that day:

- **The landing pages.** Its static pages carried no notice. The clipper page still showed "Start Clipping for Free", "No KYC. No ID check. No bank account needed." and "Works in 149+ countries, no bank required." <!-- source: https://reach.cat/become-a-clipper/ 2026-11-14 -->; its button led to the closure notice.
- **The Telegram recap.** The channel's automatic weekly recap went out on 2 and 9 November 2026, both after the closure date. Each listed 55 campaigns under "Still active" and ended "Ready to claim your share? Drop your best content & start earning" <!-- source: https://t.me/reachdotcat/350 2026-11-14 --> <!-- source: https://t.me/reachdotcat/351 2026-11-14 -->. None of the channel's last 20 posts, from 29 June to 9 November 2026, mentioned the closure <!-- source: https://t.me/s/reachdotcat 2026-11-14 -->.

Neither is an offer that can be taken up: both point to the site, and the site answers with the notice.

If you have an unpaid balance, the "Contact us" button is the only route the notice gives. Have your own figures at hand when you use it: the balance, the dates of your payout requests and whether your account had passed the identity check described below. The rest of this page is the record of what Reach.cat's terms, landing pages and app said while it ran, each fact with the date it was read. Boards that are open are compared [further down](#compare).

## The company behind Reach.cat

The terms opened with the operator: "Reach.cat is operated by REACH MARKETING NETWORK - FZCO" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/terms 2026-09-18 -->, and the privacy policy gave its address as Building A1, Dubai Digital Park, Dubai Silicon Oasis <!-- source: https://reach.cat/privacy-policy 2026-09-18 -->. The terms chose the law of the United Arab Emirates, with disputes handled "via binding arbitration or through courts in Dubai". At checkout a brand bought from a different company: orders were "sold by Inflowpay SAS, acting as the authorized reseller and Merchant of Record", incorporated in France <!-- source: https://reach.cat/terms 2026-09-18 -->.

The money ran one way. A brand funded its budget plus a 10% platform fee before launch, so a $10,000 budget cost $11,000: "$10,000 to clippers, $1,000 to Reach.cat" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/blog/reach-cat-pricing-brands-2026/ 2026-09-18 -->. The creator terms added "Reach.cat processes payouts internally" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/user-agreement 2026-09-18 -->. The party that paid a clipper was the platform, not the brand; on [Vues](/en/earnings/vues-review) it is the other way round. Neither the terms nor the creator terms, both still online, says how Reach.cat kept a prepaid budget <!-- source: https://reach.cat/terms 2026-11-14 --> <!-- source: https://reach.cat/user-agreement 2026-11-14 -->, and the closure notice does not mention those budgets <!-- source: https://reach.cat/ 2026-11-14 -->.

The terms said Reach.cat "acts strictly as a technical facilitator" and does "not vet, guarantee, or endorse" any campaign or brand, and warned that the platform "may contain offers or content that are illegal, unethical, or banned in certain countries" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/terms 2026-09-18 -->.

## What the terms said that the landing pages did not

The clipper landing page answered the usual worries in one line: "No KYC. No ID check. No bank account needed." <!-- source: https://reach.cat/become-a-clipper/ 2026-09-18 --> Another landing page promised "no selfie checks" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/new-whop-alternative/ 2026-09-18 -->, and the creator page said a clipper could "get paid without revealing your real identity" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/creator 2026-09-18 -->.

The creator terms, updated on 13 September 2026, set the limit: once a clipper's "cumulative payout requests exceed $500 USD", identity verification "is required before further withdrawals can be processed", through the partner iDenfy, and it "may require submission of a valid government-issued identification document" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/user-agreement 2026-09-18 -->. The main terms added that accounts flagged by fraud checks might need the same verification before joining public campaigns <!-- source: https://reach.cat/terms 2026-09-18 -->. So the check was deferred, not absent. The limit counted every request together: after $480 in earlier requests, a new request of $30 crossed it.

The same gap ran through four more topics:

- **The payout floor.** Two landing pages advertised cash-outs without any floor. The creator page said "Payouts are processed weekly, once your earnings reach the minimum threshold" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/creator 2026-09-18 -->, and the app's payout screen showed $5 for USDT and $10 for a bank payout (read 2026-09-18) <!-- source: https://reach.cat/dashboard/creator/earnings 2026-09-18 -->.
- **The coin.** The landing page said "USDT or SOL" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/become-a-clipper/ 2026-09-18 -->; a blog post said clippers were paid in "cryptocurrency (USDC)" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/blog/whop-clipping-vs-reach-cat/ 2026-09-18 -->; the payout screen said "Please note that only USDT will be sent."
- **Bank and PayPal.** "No bank account needed" sat beside a payout screen that took an IBAN. The agency page listed "crypto, bank transfer, PayPal" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/clipping-agencies/ 2026-09-18 -->, while the blog said "Whop supports PayPal; Reach.cat does not" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/blog/best-clipping-platforms/ 2026-09-18 -->, and the payout screen offered no PayPal at all.
- **The rate.** The clipper page promised "$1-3 CPM from day one" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/become-a-clipper/ 2026-09-18 -->, the blog quoted $1–$6 <!-- source: https://reach.cat/blog/best-clipping-platforms/ 2026-09-18 -->, and the brand page said "Most brands see CPMs between $5 and $18" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/ 2026-09-18 -->.

The fee was disputed within the blog itself: one post said "Clippers keep 100% of their CPM earnings" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/blog/best-clipping-platforms/ 2026-09-18 -->, another showed its author "Kept $280.80 after 10% fee" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/blog/tested-every-clipping-platform-2026/ 2026-09-18 -->. The terms spoke of "approximately 10% on all CPM-based payouts" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/terms 2026-09-18 -->, and the payout screen showed a $0 fee on USDT and $4 on a bank payout.

## Payout path before the closure: clearing, request, claim email

While the platform ran, the app set out its sequence in a "How payout works" panel (read 2026-09-18; the dashboard address now leads to the closure notice) <!-- source: https://reach.cat/dashboard/creator/earnings 2026-09-18 -->:

1. A clipper submitted a clip, and "The campaign manager has up to 3 days to approve it. If no action is taken, the content is automatically approved."
2. An approved clip earned "on a CPM basis, taken from the total campaign budget".
3. A "3-day clearing period" followed: "Earnings are temporarily on hold for 3 days. Our internal team reviews view activity to detect fraud or suspicious behavior."
4. "After the clearing period, you can request your payout at any time."
5. "Funds are sent within 3–5 business days after the request."

The transfer went through Payouts.com: "When your payout is approved, you will receive an email with instructions to claim your payment", and with a wrong address or network "the funds will be lost and no replacement transfer will be made". The creator terms put approved payouts at "typically" 3–5 business days after final validation and added "Reach.cat does not guarantee exact payment timing" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/user-agreement 2026-09-18 -->. On 14 November 2026 a landing page still said "Get paid in hours" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/new-whop-alternative/ 2026-11-14 -->. Neither it nor the closure notice gives a timetable for a payout that is still outstanding <!-- source: https://reach.cat/ 2026-11-14 -->.

## Rules that could take back money a clipper had earned

- **The 60-day rule.** A submitted post "must remain published, public and accessible at the URL you submitted for the whole duration of the campaign and for at least sixty (60) days after the campaign ends". If it disappeared early, "unpaid earnings are cancelled, and amounts already credited or paid out may be reversed and deducted from your balance or from your future payouts". It applied to posts submitted from 13 September 2026 <!-- source: https://reach.cat/user-agreement 2026-09-18 -->.
- **Fraud findings were final.** Suspected botting meant termination and "Loss of all funds, including pending earnings"; "Reach.cat's internal assessment is final" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/user-agreement 2026-09-18 -->.
- **No paid boosts.** A clipper could not "Boost, sponsor, whitelist, dark-post, or otherwise promote content through paid advertising", and on termination "Pending or unpaid earnings may be forfeited" <!-- source: https://reach.cat/user-agreement 2026-09-18 -->.
- **One account per person.** Multiple accounts could lead to suspension of all of them <!-- source: https://reach.cat/terms 2026-09-18 -->.

Disputes started with "good-faith informal resolution" and then proceeded individually, class and collective actions waived <!-- source: https://reach.cat/user-agreement 2026-09-18 -->.

All four rules were still in the documents under the closure notice on 14 November 2026 <!-- source: https://reach.cat/user-agreement 2026-11-14 --> <!-- source: https://reach.cat/terms 2026-11-14 -->. Whether any of them is applied after the closure, the sixty days a post must stay online among them, its pages do not say.
