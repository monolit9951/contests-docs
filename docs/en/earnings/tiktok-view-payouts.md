# What TikTok pays for views

Views on TikTok pay through two routes. The first is TikTok's own creator programme (the Creativity Program), where the platform pays you but only after you have built an audience and hold steady reach. The second is an intermediary platform like DareBay, where a named buyer pays for the views of your video out of a budget locked up front. DareBay asks for no audience at all: rates run from **$0.08 to $1.00 per 1000 views**, the median rate is **$0.50 per 1000 views**, and the typical threshold for joining the split is **2000 views** (the organizer sets the exact figure and it sits on the contest card).

Every figure on this page comes from the platform's own data (snapshot: 2026-08-04).

## Two routes to monetizing views

### The TikTok creator programme (Creativity Program)

TikTok pays creators out of its own fund. What it takes to get in:

- An established audience (tens of thousands of followers)
- Videos longer than a minute
- Consistently large reach over the past month
- A short list of eligible countries, and most of the world is not on it

Rates follow the location of your audience, your niche and the algorithms. They are opaque, they shift month to month, and TikTok publishes no exact figures.

### An intermediary platform (DareBay)

DareBay runs the other way round: a named buyer, the contest organizer, sets the rate and it stays fixed for the whole contest. In a wallet-backed contest, the budget is locked on the platform up front and payment comes out of that pool; in a manual-payout contest, the organizer pays creators directly and no money enters the DareBay wallet. No follower requirement applies. Beginner clippers compete on the same terms as experienced authors.

## The payment parameters on DareBay

| Parameter | Value | What it means |
|-----------|-------|---------------|
| Minimum rate | $0.08 per 1000 views | The low end across live contests |
| Median rate | $0.50 per 1000 views | Half of contests pay this or more |
| Maximum rate | $1.00 per 1000 views | The high end across live contests |
| View threshold | 2000 views | The median across live contests; the organizer sets the exact figure and it sits on the card |
| Payout model | PPV (pay-per-view) | Paid in proportion to views out of a locked budget |

The rate sits on the contest card, so you see the terms before you submit.

## Side by side: the TikTok programme vs an intermediary platform

| Criterion | TikTok programme | DareBay (intermediary) |
|-----------|-----------------|------------------------|
| Who pays | The platform, out of a fund | The buyer, out of a locked budget |
| Followers needed | Yes, an established audience to get in | None |
| Rate | Opaque, floating | Fixed, visible on the card |
| View threshold | A high monthly reach bar to get in | Typically 2000 on one submission |
| Payout guarantee | Rests on the fund | A wallet-backed budget is locked up front |
| Reward method | An internal TikTok balance | Defined by the contest; balance withdrawal is separate: USDT or Stars, processed manually |

## How the payout is worked out on DareBay

The formula: `(your submission's views / 1000) * the contest rate`.

At the median rate of $0.50 per 1000 views:

- 5,000 views: 5,000 / 1000 * $0.50 = **$2.50**
- 20,000 views: 20,000 / 1000 * $0.50 = **$10.00**
- 100,000 views: 100,000 / 1000 * $0.50 = **$50.00**

The typical cap per submission across live contests is **$100** (the median), and the example above remains below it. The cap applies to the submission, not to the author: enter several different clips and two of them at the cap add up to $200. The cap is on the contest card. View counts are read through the configured tikwm oracle, so neither the buyer nor the participant types figures in by hand; tikwm is not the official TikTok API.

The full chain, from locked budget to payout, is in [how pay-per-view works](/en/earnings/how-pay-per-view-works).

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need followers to take part?

No. DareBay has no minimum follower requirement. The only condition is clearing the view threshold (typically 2000, stated on the contest card) on a specific submission. That is the fundamental difference from the TikTok programme, which needs an established audience just to get in.

### What kind of videos work?

Whatever matches the brief of a specific contest. Every contest spells out the content it wants: a review, an unboxing, a challenge or another format the buyer specified. The video goes up on TikTok, or whichever platform the contest names, with the required hashtag.

### When does the money arrive?

Once the contest closes, selection and result confirmation run. The platform then records a credit or payout obligation. Fulfilment and withdrawal can be manual; the product shows the available method and status.

### Can the buyer change the rate after launch?

No. The rate is fixed when the contest is created and the organizer cannot lower it afterwards. A wallet-backed budget is frozen and the terms are fixed.

### Why is there a view threshold?

The threshold keeps the budget from being spread too thin. Without it, dozens of submissions with a handful of views each would take the budget apart penny by penny. The threshold sends the money to content that actually earns reach.

### How does the platform protect the payout obligation?

The buyer records the budget against the contest at creation. After checks, the platform calculates
amounts by formula and stores credits or obligations, while the unused remainder returns to the
buyer balance. Payout fulfilment can be manual and follows payout details and statuses.

### Is this an alternative to the TikTok programme?

It is an extra monetization channel. The TikTok programme and DareBay do not exclude each other, so use both. The difference: the programme demands an audience and pays out of a platform fund, while DareBay demands no followers and pays out of a named buyer's budget.
