# What TikTok pays for views

Views are paid for in two different ways, and it pays to tell them apart. A platform's creator programme pays you for the audience you have already built. A contest on an intermediary platform pays for the reach of one specific video: no audience needed, and you see the rate before you sit down to edit. On DareBay, live contest rates run from **$0.08 to $2.00 per 1000 views**, the median is **$0.50 per 1000 views**, and the typical threshold for entering the split is **2000 views**. The site is set by the contest: submissions are accepted on TikTok, YouTube and Instagram.

The platform's figures are a data snapshot from 2026-08-15. The TikTok and YouTube programme requirements below were **checked against the platforms' own sources on 15 August 2026**: platforms move their bars, so check them with the platform itself before you apply.

## The platform programmes: what they require in August 2026

First, what is dead. The TikTok Creator Fund is closed. The YouTube Shorts Fund is closed too, replaced by a share of ad revenue inside the YouTube Partner Program. The guides to those two funds that fill search results describe programmes that no longer exist.

**TikTok Creator Rewards Program.** This is the former Creativity Program: TikTok took it out of beta and renamed it. What it takes to get in:

- being 18 or older;
- at least 10,000 followers;
- at least 100,000 video views over the last 30 days;
- a personal account (not a business account) in good standing;
- registration in a country where the programme is open. The list is limited: the US, the UK, Germany, France, Japan, South Korea, Brazil, Mexico. Russia and the CIS countries are not on it.

The video has to qualify too: longer than a minute, original, not a duet and not a stitch, not photo mode, and not an advertisement or paid placement. TikTok publishes no fixed rate per 1000 views - the reward is worked out by a closed formula over four metrics: originality, watch duration, search value and audience engagement.

**YouTube Partner Program.** Getting in today: 1000 subscribers plus either 4000 valid public watch hours over the last 12 months, or 10 million valid Shorts views over the last 90 days. From 1 February 2027 the bar rises for new applications: 8000 hours over 365 days or 20 million Shorts views over 90 days, and to receive a share of ad and subscription revenue for Shorts a channel has to hold 10 million valid Shorts views over a rolling 90 days. Anyone already accepted into the programme is not affected by the change.

A separate point on geography: because Google's ad systems are suspended in Russia, new connections to the YouTube Partner Program from there are unavailable. Kazakhstan, Armenia and Georgia are on the list of available countries. YouTube does not publish its rate either - it depends on ad auctions and shifts month to month.

The common denominator of both programmes: build the audience first, find out what you are paid afterwards.

## A contest on an intermediary: who pays and on what terms

Here you are paid not by the site but by a specific buyer who announced a contest. DareBay stands between you and the buyer as intermediary and guarantor: in wallet-backed mode the contest budget is frozen on the platform before you take the brief, and the payout comes out of that frozen pool.

What is visible on the contest card **before you submit**: the rate per 1000 views, the view threshold, the cap on one submission, the publishing site and the brief itself. The buyer fixes the rate and the cap at creation and does not move them after launch, so you know in advance what you are playing for.

There are no follower requirements at all: the money is tied to the reach of one specific video, not to the size of a channel. A beginner clipper and an author with a million followers enter the same contest on the same terms. Taking part is free, and the platform takes no commission out of the payout.

The live sites for submissions are TikTok, YouTube and Instagram. Which one is needed is set by the individual contest, so earning from YouTube clips works here exactly like clipping for TikTok. On Instagram what counts is a video with views; a photo post gives no such metric. A submission enters the contest through the link you send to the published video; a hashtag is needed where the brief says so, and it is required for an anonymous submission.

The platform takes the views neither from your word nor from the buyer's: an independent counter reads the published view counter on the site. There is moderation and there are basic checks, and a submission with suspicious growth goes on hold.

## Six axes of comparison

| Axis | A platform creator programme | A contest on an intermediary |
|---|---|---|
| Barrier to entry | An audience already built: 10,000 followers and 100,000 views over 30 days at TikTok, 1000 subscribers plus watch hours or millions of Shorts views at YouTube | No audience needed. The threshold is counted on the individual submission, and the median across live contests is 2000 views |
| Who pays | The platform, out of its own revenue and by its own rules | A specific buyer, out of a budget frozen on the platform before the start |
| Is the rate known in advance | No. Neither TikTok nor YouTube publishes a tariff per 1000 views | Yes. The rate stands on the contest card and does not move after launch |
| How the payout is worked out | The platform's closed formula: four quality metrics at TikTok, ad auctions at YouTube | Views are divided by 1000 and multiplied by the rate. The threshold is not subtracted: once a submission clears it, the whole counter goes into the maths. Nothing is paid above the cap on one submission, and the buyer names that cap, visible on the card before you submit |
| How fast money starts | Money does not start with the first video but after the account has made up the programme's thresholds. That is months of work for nothing | The work takes part from the first video. The calculation after the contest is automatic, paying it out can be handled by hand - the platform promises no deadline |
| Availability by country | TikTok: Russia and the CIS are not on the programme's country list. YouTube: new connections from Russia are unavailable | Entry is open; the country of your account does not restrict submitting work |

## The two do not cancel each other out

This is not an either-or. A creator programme pays you for your channel, a contest pays for a specific video, and both flows can run on the same video at the same time. If an account has already been accepted into a platform's programme, taking part in contests does not break that.

The order that works in practice: contests pay money right away while the channel is still small, and at the same time bring it views and followers - exactly what is needed to get into a creator programme. Once the platform's thresholds are cleared, the programme is added on top rather than replacing contests.

One check before counting on both at once: a creator programme has its own requirements for the video itself, and it may not count a paid placement. Read a specific programme's terms with the platform, and a specific contest's terms on its card.

## Frequently asked questions

### What should I choose if I have no audience yet?

Contests. A creator programme is unavailable on a zero channel for pure arithmetic reasons: TikTok's 10,000 followers or YouTube's first thresholds have to be reached, and the platform pays nothing for the whole of that time. A contest pays for the reach of a video rather than the size of a channel, so a first payout is possible from the very first submission that clears the threshold.

### Where is the rate higher?

There is nothing to compare directly: neither TikTok nor YouTube publishes a tariff per 1000 views, and for both it is the output of a closed formula and ad auctions. The honest comparison runs along a different line - in a contest the rate is known before the work starts, while with a platform it is known only once the money arrives.

### The programme is unavailable in my country. Are contests too?

No, those are different restrictions. The list of countries for a creator programme is set by the platform itself, and Russia and the CIS countries are not on it. Taking part in contests on DareBay is not restricted by the country of your account; the available ways to get the money are covered in the payout breakdown.

### Which site should I publish the work on?

The one the contest named. The platform currently counts views for TikTok, YouTube and Instagram, the specific site is chosen by the buyer at contest creation, and it is stated on the card. Publishing on a different site does not count.

### Is it worth waiting until I have an audience and only then starting?

There is no point. Programme thresholds move up, not down: YouTube doubles the bar for new applications from 1 February 2027. Waiting for a way into a programme while earning nothing along the way is the worst of the available scenarios.
