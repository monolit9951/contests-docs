## Who is behind Vues and who owes you the money

On Vues a brand sets a rate per 1,000 views and a budget, and clippers post to TikTok, Instagram, YouTube or X and submit the link <!-- source: https://vues.app/terms 2026-09-18 -->. The terms name the other party only as "Vues" and place the agreement under the laws of the State of Delaware <!-- source: https://vues.app/terms 2026-09-18 -->. The privacy policy adds "We are based in, and process information in, the United States" <!-- source: https://vues.app/privacy 2026-09-18 -->, and the seller of its iPhone app is Vues Technology LLC <!-- source: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vues-the-clipper-app/id6788968167 2026-09-18 -->.

Section 1 of the terms settles the main question: "The Brand — not Vues — is responsible for paying the Clipper." Vues describes itself as "a neutral venue and intermediary", and section 7 says campaign and payout funds "remain owned and controlled by the Brand" and "are not deposited with or custodied by Vues" <!-- source: https://vues.app/terms 2026-09-18 -->. A payout request you file "is an administrative instruction and does not itself move funds". For a crypto payout Vues only passes your wallet address on; the brand's own wallet, run on Privy infrastructure, sends the transfer <!-- source: https://vues.app/terms 2026-09-18 -->.

Vues may ask a brand to show reserved funds before launch, but promises nothing: "We do not guarantee that any campaign, earnings, results, content, or Payout will be available, approved, or successful" <!-- source: https://vues.app/terms 2026-09-18 -->. [Reach.cat](/en/earnings/reach-cat-review) is built the other way round: there the platform pays clippers out of a budget the brand prepays.

## Where Vues campaigns come from

Rates are not public: "Exact CPM rates and briefs live inside the app — join free to see them" <!-- source: https://vues.app/campaigns 2026-09-18 -->. The one public number is an example: the calculator on the homepage works at $3.00 per 1,000 views <!-- source: https://vues.app/ 2026-09-18 -->.

What Vues does publish is a directory of 18 organizations with all-time totals as of July 2026. Roobet leads with 8.70B views tracked and $600k+ paid to clippers, followed by Duel with $580k+, Polymarket with $150k+ and CrownCoins with $110k+ <!-- source: https://vues.app/campaigns 2026-09-18 -->. Vues' own descriptions place the list in one corner of the market: CrownCoins is "a social casino brand" <!-- source: https://vues.app/campaigns/crowncoins 2026-09-18 -->, Rebet "the social sports betting app" <!-- source: https://vues.app/campaigns/rebet 2026-09-18 -->, Novig "the commission-free sports prediction market" <!-- source: https://vues.app/campaigns/novig 2026-09-18 -->. Its blog sums the roster up as "weighted toward crypto, prediction markets, betting and gaming" <!-- source: https://vues.app/blog/vues-vs-vyro 2026-09-18 -->.

The terms require you to be at least 18 <!-- source: https://vues.app/terms 2026-09-18 -->, and whether promoting a casino or a sportsbook is lawful where you and your viewers live is your question, not the platform's. The public list is not the whole board either: the totals "span all 60+ funded brands, including programs that run privately" <!-- source: https://vues.app/campaigns 2026-09-18 -->.

Platform-wide, as of July 2026, Vues reports "$3M+ Paid to clippers" and 2,933 clippers <!-- source: https://vues.app/campaigns 2026-09-18 -->, and its blog admits the top ten account for "close to a third of everything ever paid out" <!-- source: https://vues.app/blog/clipping-earnings-real-numbers 2026-09-18 -->.

## From tracked view to balance: caps and the end-of-campaign freeze

The terms define the arithmetic: verified views divided by 1,000, multiplied by the campaign's rate, accruing "until the Campaign's budget cap or end date is reached". Which views count is Vues' decision, taken from the social platforms "and from our own tracking, which controls over any other count" <!-- source: https://vues.app/terms 2026-09-18 -->.

There is no view floor on a clip: Vues' own comparison table gives "Minimum view threshold per clip" as "None" <!-- source: https://vues.app/blog/best-clipping-platforms-that-pay-crypto 2026-09-18 -->. A campaign can instead set a minimum payout per qualifying post, a maximum per post, and limits per profile or per poster, and "If your post hits a cap, your effective payout is clamped to it" <!-- source: https://vues.app/blog/how-cpm-payouts-work 2026-09-18 -->.

The counting window is the campaign itself. A clip has to be "posted inside the campaign window" <!-- source: https://vues.app/blog/how-clipping-platforms-verify-views 2026-09-18 -->, and "When a campaign ends, your earnings freeze at the view counts as of end-time" <!-- source: https://vues.app/blog/how-cpm-payouts-work 2026-09-18 -->. Views after the close still reach the brand's report, while "your payout stays fixed at end-time" <!-- source: https://vues.app/blog/how-cpm-payouts-work 2026-09-18 -->.

## Cashing out: rails, the ERC-20 network and the fee

The homepage lists five rails: "Withdraw to USDT, Solana, BTC, PayPal, or Bank Transfer" <!-- source: https://vues.app/ 2026-09-18 -->. The terms are narrower and conditional: payouts go through "the methods a Brand makes available through the Service, which may include cryptocurrency (such as USDT), PayPal, or Zelle" <!-- source: https://vues.app/terms 2026-09-18 -->. The iPhone app names a single network: "Set a USDT (ERC-20) address once", with "Additional payout methods are available on vues.app" <!-- source: https://vues.app/ios 2026-09-18 -->. A USDT withdrawal from the app therefore needs an Ethereum address.

A minimum exists and is not published. The homepage speaks of cashing out "once you clear the minimum withdrawal" <!-- source: https://vues.app/ 2026-09-18 -->, and the blog adds "Your current balance and the threshold are both visible in your account" <!-- source: https://vues.app/blog/clipping-payout-methods 2026-09-18 -->.

The fee is not published as a rate either. The homepage FAQ says "Vues takes its cut from the brand's budget. The only fee you see is a small processing fee on withdrawals, shown before you confirm" <!-- source: https://vues.app/ 2026-09-18 -->. The referral page works through a $1,000 cash-out on which "Vues takes its 10% platform fee", labelled "Illustrative" and assuming the standard fee, and adds that referred clippers "pay exactly the same fee whether or not they signed up through you" <!-- source: https://vues.app/affiliate 2026-09-18 -->. The number that binds you is the one on the confirmation screen.

The terms allow "identity, tax, or payout-information verification" before a payout and mention Form W-9 or W-8 <!-- source: https://vues.app/terms 2026-09-18 -->; the blog names the usual reason a withdrawal is held: "Payout accounts that don't match your platform profile" <!-- source: https://vues.app/blog/clipping-payout-methods 2026-09-18 -->. There is no country list, by choice: "We don't publish a country list because these change" <!-- source: https://vues.app/blog/clipping-from-any-country 2026-09-18 -->.

## Six things Vues says two different ways

1. **Whether payment is guaranteed.** The homepage calls its campaigns "fully funded with a 100% guarantee for payout" <!-- source: https://vues.app/ 2026-09-18 --> and the App Store description says "payouts are guaranteed" <!-- source: https://apps.apple.com/us/app/vues-the-clipper-app/id6788968167 2026-09-18 -->; the terms guarantee no payout at all.
2. **Who sends the payout.** The brands page says "We split and send every payout in crypto, PayPal, or bank transfer" <!-- source: https://vues.app/brands 2026-09-18 -->; the terms say Vues does not "transmit the funds used for a Clipper's Payout".
3. **What the clipper pays.** "A small processing fee" on the homepage, a 10% platform fee in the referral example.
4. **Which rails exist.** Five on the homepage, whatever the brand switches on in the terms, one network in the app.
5. **When you are paid.** The homepage promises "No waiting on a pay cycle" <!-- source: https://vues.app/ 2026-09-18 -->; the creator sign-in page says "Paid every Friday" <!-- source: https://vues.app/login/creator 2026-09-18 -->; the blog says "Earnings settle automatically when a campaign ends" <!-- source: https://vues.app/blog/clipping-payout-methods 2026-09-18 -->.
6. **Whether you apply.** The homepage steps read "Apply, get approved, and review post requirements" <!-- source: https://vues.app/ 2026-09-18 -->; a comparison post in its blog promises "no application, no follower minimum" <!-- source: https://vues.app/blog/vues-vs-vyro 2026-09-18 -->.

Take numbers from the terms and the confirmation screen, not from the marketing.

## Rejections, fraud flags and arbitration

"The Brand and Vues may approve or reject any Submission, and a rejected or non-compliant Submission is not eligible for a Payout." Bought or automated views "do not count and are not compensable", a post without an #Ad-style disclosure can be marked ineligible, and a brand "may delay, withhold, or seek recovery of a Payout" on suspicion of fraud, a chargeback or a refund <!-- source: https://vues.app/terms 2026-09-18 -->. The blog adds the usual causes: a broken brief, an unlinked account, a duplicate, a watermark, an authenticity flag, or a clip that "landed after the campaign closed or the budget ran out" <!-- source: https://vues.app/blog/why-clips-get-rejected 2026-09-18 -->.

Against an automated fraud decision the privacy policy offers one route: "you may contact us to request human review or to contest a decision" <!-- source: https://vues.app/privacy 2026-09-18 -->. A larger dispute starts with 30 days of informal talks through support@vues.app and then goes to binding individual arbitration, class actions waived, unless you opt out by email within 30 days of accepting the terms <!-- source: https://vues.app/terms 2026-09-18 -->.

DareBay takes 0% from contest payouts. The team checks and sends every withdrawal, so no fixed time is promised <!-- source: https://darebay.com/en/help/darebay-withdrawals 2026-09-24 -->: [how withdrawals work](/en/help/darebay-withdrawals), [what DareBay charges](/en/help/what-commission).

Vues fits better in these cases:

- **You want PayPal, a bank transfer or USDT on a chain other than TON.** Vues' homepage lists PayPal and bank transfer and its app pays USDT on ERC-20; a DareBay balance leaves in USDT on TON, or you receive the payout in Telegram Stars; a card or bank transfer comes only from a prize contest that names it as the reward <!-- source: https://darebay.com/en/help/prizes-and-payouts 2026-09-24 -->.
- **You want no view floor on a clip.** Vues' own table lists none; on DareBay each task sets its threshold.
- **You clip crypto, betting or prediction-market content and want many funded programs to choose from.** Vues shows 18 organizations, the largest with six-figure payout totals, and says more run privately.

The other side of the ledger is custody. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is Vues a scam or a real platform?

A real business: US privacy policy, Delaware terms, an App Store listing under Vues Technology LLC, published payout totals. The risk is structural: the brand, not Vues, owes you each payout, and the terms guarantee none.

### Does Vues hold the campaign budget?

No. Its terms keep campaign money with the brand, even in a Privy-managed wallet; Vues can only ask a brand to show that funds are reserved before a campaign goes live.

### What network does Vues send USDT on?

ERC-20, on Ethereum, is the only network it names, in its iPhone app. The homepage lists Solana and BTC without networks, and the terms leave crypto to the brand.

### How much does Vues take when you withdraw?

It publishes no rate: a small processing fee on the homepage, a 10% platform fee in the referral example. The confirmation screen shows the one that applies to you.

### Which day does Vues pay?

Its pages give three answers: on demand, every Friday, or at the end of the campaign. In every case the brand has to fund and authorize the payout.

### Do views after a Vues campaign closes earn anything?

No. Earnings freeze at the view count at the campaign's end time; later views appear in the brand's report but are not paid.

### Does Vues ask for a W-9 or ID?

It can: the terms allow identity, tax or payout-information checks, name Form W-9 or W-8, and leave any Form 1099 to the paying brand or its payment provider.
