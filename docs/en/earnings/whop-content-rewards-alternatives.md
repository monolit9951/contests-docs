## Build a shortlist you can actually withdraw from

Check recipient eligibility before comparing advertised CPM: residence, age, tax status where required and a payout account you control. Then open a particular brief and record its model, remaining budget, cap and approval requirements. A marketplace listing is not an offer you have already been accepted into.

Use the updated [Vues review](/en/earnings/vues-review) to distinguish bank or USDC payouts through Stripe from USDT, and the [Prime review](/en/earnings/prime-oracles-review) to separate short clips from longer review briefs. Older campaign examples retain their dates. Reach.cat's current terms could not be fully re-read, so confirm its payout details directly before relying on a historical row. Keep two sources of briefs only when you can manage their rules and avoid double submitting the same work.

## Why clippers look beyond Whop Content Rewards

Whop Content Rewards is where most clipping campaigns live: brands fund CPM campaigns, Whop keeps the brand's budget on its side of the campaign, and clippers are paid per verified view into a Content Rewards wallet that is withdrawn through Whop <!-- source: https://contentrewards.com/terms 2026-09-18 -->. Four things send clippers looking for an alternative, and each of them is written on Whop's own pages.

**Countries.** Whop's sanctioned-country list blocks registration and payouts for Russia and Belarus, among others, and for six regions of Ukraine: Crimea, Sevastopol, Donetsk, Luhansk, Zaporizhzhia and Kherson; Kazakhstan is not listed. A clipper in those places cannot be paid there at all.

**The fee.** Whop's terms take a flat 10% from CPM payouts, and the creator FAQ now names the same flat 10%, with no tiers <!-- source: https://contentrewards.com/creators 2026-09-18 -->. The rate on the campaign card is still not the rate that reaches you.

**Churn.** Brand campaigns start at $1,000 and stop the moment the budget is spent, so a clipper who relies on one marketplace spends time waiting for the next brief.

**Timing.** The first CPM round opens when approval and the minimum are both met; later rounds can open after the preceding round settles while eligible earnings remain. A round normally has seven earning days and a three-day validation hold.<!-- source: https://contentrewards.com/terms 2026-10-12 -->

The sanctioned list is not the only filter, either: Whop publishes a separate list of the countries it pays out to, and its payout setup ends in identity verification — [clipping platforms that pay without PayPal or Stripe](/en/earnings/clipping-platforms-that-pay-without-paypal-or-stripe) weighs alternatives on exactly those two points.

## Choose the alternative by your situation

It needs no followers, a wallet-backed contest locks its budget on the platform before the start, and you are paid in USDT on TON, or receive the payout in Telegram Stars<!-- source: https://darebay.com/en/help/darebay-withdrawals 2026-09-24 -->, so check that you can receive USDT where you live. Klipni pays in rubles but requires Russian self-employed status. Prime Oracles names Russia, Belarus and Kazakhstan among ten CIS states in its agreement's tax clause, publishes no list of served countries, and pays in USDT or USDC from $5<!-- source: https://www.primeoracles.com/terms-agreement 2026-11-14 -->. Vyro is not an option: Russia, Belarus and the whole of Ukraine are on its list of 28 countries it does not pay<!-- source: https://vyro.com/help/earnings-and-payments/eligibility 2026-11-14 -->, and since 2 November 2026 its cash-outs have gone to a Whop wallet<!-- source: https://vyro.com/changelog/more-ways-to-get-paid-with-whop 2026-11-14 -->. Try Shoot is not one either: Russia, Belarus and Ukraine are among the 26 countries on its restricted list, where its terms say accounts and payouts are not available<!-- source: https://tryshoot.com/terms 2026-11-14 -->. ## Frequently asked questions

### Is there a Whop alternative that pays clippers in Russia?

Yes. DareBay pays clippers in Russia, Belarus, Ukraine, Kazakhstan and the rest of CIS in USDT on TON or Telegram Stars. Klipni pays in rubles to Russian self-employed clippers. Prime Oracles, which pays in crypto, names Russia, Belarus and Kazakhstan in its agreement's tax clause. Vyro and Whop both exclude Russia, and so does Try Shoot, whose restricted list also names Belarus and Ukraine<!-- source: https://tryshoot.com/terms 2026-11-14 -->. Clipster says it does not block creators by country<!-- source: https://www.clipster.com/help-centre/getting-started/how-do-i-set-up-my-account-and-link-my-socials 2026-11-14 -->, but its music campaigns do not accept posts in Russian<!-- source: https://www.clipster.com/help-centre/campaigns-and-rules/what-do-logo-and-music-campaigns-require 2026-11-14 -->.

### Which Whop alternative has the lowest fee?

Klipni publishes a 5% fee on approved payouts. Vues and Reach.cat give no single answer: each one's own pages disagree on whether its cut comes from the brand or from the clipper's payout <!-- source: https://vues.app/affiliate 2026-09-18 --> <!-- source: https://reach.cat/terms 2026-09-18 -->. DareBay charges 0% on contest payouts; withdrawals carry a default 10% fee, with a possible account-specific rate. Requests start at 10 USDT, and the form shows the actual fee and amount to receive before confirmation. Vyro says it charges nothing to send a cash-out to your Whop wallet, where Whop's own payout fees apply<!-- source: https://vyro.com/help/earnings-and-payments/payouts 2026-11-14 -->. Clipster takes 5% of each withdrawal once your balance reaches $50<!-- source: https://www.clipster.com/help-centre/earnings-and-payouts/how-do-i-withdraw-my-earnings-and-what-are-the-fees 2026-11-14 -->, and Try Shoot 7% plus the cost of the transfer, with a $10 minimum<!-- source: https://tryshoot.com/help/shoot-fees-explained 2026-11-14 -->. Elev8or's pages disagree with each other on its creator fee<!-- source: https://www.elev8or.io/help/creators/getting-paid 2026-11-14 --> <!-- source: https://www.elev8or.io/refund 2026-11-14 -->, and Promote.fun's terms mention platform fees without a rate<!-- source: https://www.promote.fun/terms-of-service/ 2026-09-24 -->. An archive copy of Clipping.net's FAQ from 17 September 2026 says you keep your earnings minus any team commission you opt into<!-- source: https://web.archive.org/web/20260917191233/https://clipping.net/clip 2026-09-17 -->.

### Can I use Whop and another platform at the same time?

Yes. Nothing on Whop's pages forbids clipping for other marketplaces, and campaigns on every platform run out of budget, so two or three sources of briefs keep you working. Check each campaign's exclusivity rules before posting the same clip twice.

### Is DareBay a Whop alternative?

Yes, especially where Whop cannot pay: DareBay keeps no country list, and only people on sanctions lists are barred<!-- source: https://darebay.com/en/legal/terms 2026-09-24 -->. Its pay-per-view tasks pay at the task-specific rate and cap, with no follower requirement<!-- source: https://darebay.com/en/earnings/how-much-clipping-pays 2026-09-24 -->, and a wallet-backed contest locks its budget on the platform before the start<!-- source: https://darebay.com/en/about/payout-guarantee 2026-09-24 -->. Offer tasks can add a fixed fee per accepted clip and a share of sales, and hand you ready-made videos with built-in uniqueization.

### What happened to ClipAffiliates?

On the day we checked, clipaffiliates.com answered "service suspended" and its bare domain pointed nowhere, so it is not in this comparison. Its archived pages described a 9% fee and Stripe payouts; we will add it back if it returns.
