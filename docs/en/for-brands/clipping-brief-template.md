## The template to copy

Copy it into the contest description, replace the bracketed notes with your details and delete what you do not need, except the three numbers: the platform asks for them separately anyway.

```text
GOAL: (one line: what the viewer should do after the clip)
MATERIALS: (folder link with footage, logo, examples; or "clips come from the platform")
FORMAT AND LENGTH: (vertical 9:16, N to N seconds, subtitles yes/no, language)
PLATFORMS: (TikTok / Reels / Shorts; which are accepted)
HASHTAG: (needed or not; if so, the exact tag and where it goes)
MUST BE IN THE CLIP: (what a clip is not accepted without)
NOT ALLOWED: (the list a clip is rejected for)
RATE / THRESHOLD / CAP: ($N per 1,000 views / N views / $N per clip)
DEADLINE: (date, time and time zone submissions close)
HOW WORK IS ACCEPTED: (published link, moderator check, what gets rejected)
```

## Field by field: what to write and why

**Goal.** One line about the viewer's action: install the app, follow the link in the bio, remember the name. Without one, a clipper shoots whatever is easiest.

**Materials.** A folder link that opens without an access request: footage, logo, two or three clips you like. If the platform provides ready-made uniqueized clips, say so: no editing means faster pickup.

**Format and length.** Vertical 9:16, a range of seconds, subtitles, language. "20 to 45 seconds" leaves room to edit; "exactly 30" forces the creator to cut meaning.

**Platforms.** Where the clip must go out. Views come from the public counter of the site the clip was published on, so the platform is a term, not a wish.

**Hashtag.** The platform does not collect clips by hashtag; a submission enters only through the creator's link. Ask for a tag only if you want the clips findable in search.

**Must be in the clip.** What the moderator and you check against: the product screen for at least N seconds, the name spoken or on screen, the link in the description.

**Not allowed.** The list a clip is rejected for: other people's clips, promises of income, shock content, music without rights, naming competitors. A ban missing from the brief cannot be applied afterwards.

**Rate, threshold, cap.** The three numbers on the card: open tasks pay $1 to $2 per 1,000 views, the threshold defaults to 1,000 and is typically 2,000, the cap per clip is usually $100. What each number buys: [clipping campaign cost](/en/for-brands/clipping-campaign-cost). Your rate from the creator's side: [earnings calculator](/en/earnings/clipping-earnings-calculator); what they compare you with: [platform comparison](/en/earnings/best-clipping-platforms).

**Deadline.** Date, time and time zone: "by the end of the week" means something else to a clipper elsewhere.

**How work is accepted.** The creator publishes and sends the link, the moderator checks it against the terms, you reject non-matching work in the selection window, the platform counts the views; a rejected clip is not paid.

## A filled-in example

A hypothetical brief for a habit-tracking app, call it DayByDay.

```text
GOAL: the viewer sees that the app counts habit streaks and installs it via the link in the bio.
MATERIALS: folder with five screen recordings, logo, three reference clips; editing is on the creator.
FORMAT AND LENGTH: vertical 9:16, 20 to 45 seconds, subtitles required, language English.
PLATFORMS: TikTok, Reels, Shorts only.
HASHTAG: #daybyday in the description, first.
MUST BE IN THE CLIP: the app screen for at least 5 seconds, the name spoken or on screen, a 7-day streak.
NOT ALLOWED: other people's clips and stock recordings, promises like "changes your life in a week", medical claims, naming other apps.
RATE / THRESHOLD / CAP: $1 per 1,000 views / 2,000 views / $100 per clip.
DEADLINE: 30 September, 23:59 UTC; clips published before the launch are not accepted.
HOW WORK IS ACCEPTED: published link through the task form; the moderator checks both lists; a clip without the app screen is rejected and not paid.
```

## Five phrasings that bring the wrong clips

1. **"Make a viral clip about our product."** A wish, not a goal: the creator shoots what goes viral in their niche and the product ends up as background.
2. **A brief with no bans.** You get other people's clips under your hashtag, clickbait promises and music the site will mute, and nothing to reject them for.
3. **No example.** "In our style" means nothing to someone who has never seen the brand. Two links you like and one you do not beat a paragraph of adjectives.
4. **"Like our competitor."** The creator repeats the other campaign, including what did not work, and you pay for a copy instead of unique clips.
5. **"As soon as possible."** Without a date the clipper has no reason to put your task first, and you have no grounds to reject a late clip.

## Checking the brief before you publish

1. The goal fits in one line and describes the viewer's action.
2. The materials link opens in a private window.
3. Format, length and platforms are numbers and names, with no "etc.".
4. Every point on both lists is specific enough to reject a particular clip.
5. Rate, threshold and cap are numbers checked against the budget; the first two cannot change after the start.
6. It is clear how the creator sends the work and what happens to a rejected clip.

Then it goes into the form: [how to set up a contest](/en/for-brands/set-up-a-clipping-contest) shows where each field goes; [how to pay clippers](/en/for-brands/paying-clippers-per-view) covers the money side, [how to commission clips](/en/for-brands/commission-clips-for-a-campaign) the contest-versus-hiring comparison, and the [business page](/en/for-business) the formats for business.

## Frequently asked questions

### Is a hashtag required in the brief?

No. A submission enters the task through the creator's link; the platform does not collect clips by hashtag. Ask for one only if you want the clips findable in search, and write it exactly.

### How much source material should clippers get?

Enough for a choice, not a search: five to ten fragments of one or two minutes, the logo, two or three references. With no footage, say so and describe what must be in the frame.

### Does one template work for TikTok, Reels and Shorts?

Yes: all three take a vertical clip, and views on each are read from its public counter. The differences are mostly length, so set a range under "Format" that fits each.

### What if a clip matches the brief on paper but is not what I wanted?

If the brief has a point you can cite, the work is rejected in the selection window and not paid. If not, the clip is accepted on your terms and paid by the formula: add the ban to the next brief rather than arguing.
