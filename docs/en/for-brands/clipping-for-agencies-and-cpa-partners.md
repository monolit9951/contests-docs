An agency works with clippers in three models: a markup on the per-view rate, a retainer for managing campaigns, or a clips-plus-traffic package where it sells a result rather than hours. On DareBay all three sit the same way: task, budget and records live on the platform; the agency owns the client, terms and review.

## Three ways an agency works with clippers

**A markup on the per-view rate.** The client pays the agency a price per 1000 views; the agency sets creators a lower rate and keeps the difference. It creates the task, funds the budget and gets a view count per clip. The creators' rate is visible on the task card, so the markup rests on the contract.

**A retainer for campaign management.** The client pays a fixed monthly fee for the running: task terms, reviewing submissions, reports, while the creator budget passes through one to one. The task can run from either account.

**A clips-plus-traffic package.** The agency sells a result: so many clips plus views, clicks or sales. Both platform tools work inside: a pay-per-view task brings reach, a CPA offer brings sales tracked per creator. The margin is higher, and so is the risk.

| Model | Who carries the reach risk | What the client sees | When it fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| Markup on the rate | the client: pays per view | the price per 1000 and a per-clip report | one-off campaigns, niche tests |
| Retainer | the client: budget passes one to one | everything, including the creators' rate | regular clients, several campaigns monthly |
| Clips plus traffic | the agency: a result is promised | the package outcome and a sales report | clients with an offer and a clear action |

## CPA offers through the creator network

For a client with an offer the agency runs sales, not only views. A creator on such a task gets a fixed fee per accepted clip plus a share of the sales from their link or promo code. Each creator gets a tracked link, the offer may carry a promo code, conversions arrive by S2S postback into the tracker, and any order traces to a specific clip. Incentivized, bot and pop traffic are banned: such conversions are not paid and the submission is rejected.

A separate tool for offers is ready-made videos with built-in uniqueization: creators need no editing and the copies do not repeat each other, so one setup approved by the client spreads across dozens of accounts. The brand and affiliate-network side is on the [partners page](/en/partners); traffic partners come in through the [traffic section](/en/earn/traffic).

## What the agency gets from the platform

- **View counting.** Views are read from the public counter of the site each clip went out on, several times a day.
- **Moderation.** A submission counts only after its link is sent and a moderator checks it against the terms; fraud is filtered out, not paid.
- **Creator payouts.** In wallet-backed mode the budget is locked on the platform and goes out by formula: views / 1000 × rate, up to the cap; in manual mode the agency pays from the statement.
- **Per-creator and per-clip records.** Submissions, links, counted views and credits per creator: the client report is built from this.

It does not hand-pick creators, guarantee turnout or promise a payout timeline; that is the agency's job.

## How to work out the economics

One formula covers every model: the client price per 1000 views is the creator rate times one plus the markup.

> client price = creator rate × (1 + markup)

A hypothetical example: the client's budget is $2 000, the creator rate $1.00 per 1000 views (open tasks currently run from $1.00 to $2.00 per 1000 views), the markup, say, 25%. The client price is then $1.25 per 1000, and the budget buys 2 000 / 1.25 × 1000 = 1 600 000 counted views. Creators receive 1 600 000 / 1000 × $1.00 = **$1 600.00**, the agency keeps **$400.00**, plus 10 USDT for contest creation. The threshold, typically 2000, filters out clips with no reach; the cap per submission, typically $100, spreads the money across at least 16 clips. ## How to get started

1. **Collect material and goal from the client.** Recordings, videos, the product; for an offer, the tracking link, payout terms and restrictions.
2. **Create the task.** From either account: terms, sites, rate per 1000 views, threshold, cap, budget. Contest creation costs 10 USDT once, the fee is 0%. Step by step: [how to set up a clipping contest](/en/for-brands/set-up-a-clipping-contest).
3. **Fund the budget.** In wallet-backed mode the amount is locked on the platform before launch; in manual mode you pay creators yourself from the statement.
4. **Review submissions.** Reject clips that break the terms; the platform counts views and credits.
5. **Report to the client.** Links, counted views and amounts per creator come from the task; launch price and remainder are covered in [how to pay clippers per view](/en/for-brands/paying-clippers-per-view) and [what a clipping campaign costs](/en/for-brands/clipping-campaign-cost).
6. **For a CPA offer, write through the partners page.** Send the offer, payout terms and guidelines; a control batch comes first, then scale. Other formats: the [business page](/en/for-business).

## Common questions

### Does the client see the rate creators are paid?

Yes, if they open the task: the rate, threshold and cap stand on the card for everyone. A markup therefore works only with a transparent contract; if the client wants to see everything, a retainer is the more honest model.

### Who pays the creators on a CPA offer, the agency or the platform?

On an offer the platform runs, the platform pays the creators, and the advertiser or network settles with it under the offer terms. If the agency launches the task itself, its own budget pays.

### How does the platform treat incentivized traffic on an offer?

The submission is rejected and its conversions are not paid: incentivized traffic is banned alongside bots and pop traffic, and the tag on each creator and clip shows where an order came from.

### Can an agency run tasks for several clients at once?

Yes. Each task lives separately, with its own budget, terms, submission list and credits, so clients' campaigns do not mix.
