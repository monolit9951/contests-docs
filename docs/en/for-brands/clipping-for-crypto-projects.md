## Why crypto lives in short clips

Crypto audiences discover projects in the feed, not on banners, and a forty-second explanation of what a protocol does reaches them faster than a landing page. Four formats work consistently for clippers, and all four come from material a project already has.

- **The explainer.** What the product is and who it is for, one idea per clip: "how a non-custodial swap works".
- **The news clip.** A listing, a release, an integration, a partnership: short-lived but fast.
- **The tutorial.** The product screen and the user's path: sign up, deposit, swap, withdraw. People search for it, so it has the longest tail.
- **The AMA cut.** One minute out of an hour with the founder: one answer to one question.

The clipper publishes on their own TikTok, Reels or Shorts account and sends the link; you get dozens of takes on the product with no ad account and no sponsored label.

## Brief rules that protect the project

In crypto a brief without rules is riskier than no campaign: one clip promising "100x" under your hashtag is a regulator's complaint and a banned creator account. Write six rules into the Not-allowed field word for word.

1. **No promises of returns.** No "guaranteed yield", "100x by listing" or price-forecast charts: the clip is about the product, not the token price.
2. **No signals.** No "buy now", "get in before the announcement", timeframes or price targets: that is investment advice, and the platforms remove it with the account.
3. **A disclaimer in the frame or the description.** "Not financial advice" and "crypto assets can lose value" in one line; give the exact wording.
4. **Official links only.** The site, the app and the channels on your list; a "mirror" or a bot from the comments is rejected without pay.
5. **No third-party referral links.** Referral links to exchanges and other services are banned; your own tag per creator is issued by you.
6. **Within the platforms' rules.** TikTok, Instagram and YouTube restrict financial and crypto advertising: the clip has to read as an explanation or a tutorial, not a call to invest.

A clip that breaks any of the six is rejected in the selection window and is not paid; the fields this goes into are covered in the [clipping brief template](/en/for-brands/clipping-brief-template).

## Pay per view in USDT

For a crypto project the money side is already in USDT: the budget is funded in USDT and creators receive USDT to a wallet on the TON network. You set a rate per 1,000 views, a threshold, a cap per clip and a budget; the platform reads views from the site's public counter, reconciles several times a day and filters out inflated views.

In open tasks rates currently run from $1 to $2 per 1,000 views. The threshold defaults to 1,000 views and is typically 2,000: a clip below it costs nothing, a clip above it is paid for its whole counter. The cap per clip is usually $100: a viral clip gets the maximum, the rest goes to other creators. A hypothetical calculation:

> 20,000 / 1,000 × $1.00 = **$20.00**

Creating a contest costs 10 USDT once, the contest fee is 0% and topping up the budget is 0%: the pool goes to creators in full. In a wallet-backed task the budget is locked on the platform before launch, spent on counted views, and the remainder returns to your balance.

Once a submission is approved, the creator can lock in what it has earned and withdraw USDT to their TON wallet: the minimum withdrawal is 10 USDT, the withdrawal fee is 10% of the requested amount, processing is manual. In manual mode the organizer pays creators directly from the platform's statement, in Telegram Stars or to a TON wallet. The full campaign cost is on the [clipping campaign cost](/en/for-brands/clipping-campaign-cost) page.

## Two formats: the explainer and the product tutorial

For a first task take the two formats that are easiest to check and least likely to break platform rules.

| | Explainer | Product tutorial |
|---|---|---|
| What is in the clip | one idea: what the product does and for whom | the user's path on screen from sign-in to result |
| Materials from you | key points, glossary, logo, links | test account or demo mode, screen recording, current app version |
| Length | 20 to 45 seconds | 30 to 60 seconds |
| Must be in the clip | project name, disclaimer, official link in the description | product screen for at least half the clip, name, disclaimer |
| Rejected for | price forecast, signal, third-party referral link | old interface, hidden steps, another app in the frame |

Shoot the tutorial on a test account without real funds, so nobody's balances end up in the frame. For the explainer, provide a glossary: the same terms across ten clips build recognition, different ones build confusion.

## Community and ambassadors

A crypto project already has people who explain the product in the chat for free. A pay-per-view task turns them into clippers: they know the product, and a public view counter gives them honest pay instead of one-off "activity" rewards.

Publish the task, then post in your Telegram and Discord with a link and two lines: the rate per 1,000 views and what is banned. Give ambassadors their own tag on the link, so you see clicks per creator, and materials ahead of everyone else. Do not promise them tokens for clips: the platform counts and pays only USDT for views, and a token promise in the brief breaks your own rule one.

The community is the first circle, not the ceiling: creators worldwide see the task, with the largest community in the CIS; more ways to gather them are on [how to find clippers](/en/for-brands/how-to-find-clippers). Projects that live in Telegram have the [Telegram projects vertical](/en/for-business/telegram), wallets and exchanges with a mobile app the [apps vertical](/en/for-business/apps).

## What to measure

**Counted views.** Each submission shows the link, the counted views and the amount earned; keep the format that collected more views per clip in the next task.

**Rejections.** Track which of the six rules you reject for most often; if it is signals and forecasts, the creators did not see the ban, so move it to the first line of the brief.

**Clicks by tag.** A tag per creator shows who brought sign-ups, a promo code shows deposits. The platform counts views only; the rest lives in your own analytics and decides whom to invite to the next task first.

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I pay clippers in my own token?

No: the platform counts and pays in USDT, by the formula in wallet-backed mode or from the statement in manual mode, to a TON wallet or in Telegram Stars. A bonus in your own token is neither counted nor guaranteed by the platform, and promising it in the brief breaks your own rules.

### How do crypto clips get through platform moderation?

By format: an explanation or a tutorial, not a call to invest, with no prices, forecasts or "buy now", plus a disclaimer and an official link. A clip that passes your rule list usually passes TikTok's moderation too.

### Is a referral link per creator required?

No, but it is useful: your own tag shows sign-ups per creator. Third-party referral links are banned; your own link is issued by you and goes into the must-be-in-the-clip field or the clip description.

### What if the market drops during the campaign?

Clips about the product rather than the price keep working in a downturn: a withdrawal tutorial is exactly what viewers need then. The budget is spent only on counted views, and a task that is no longer relevant can be paused: new submissions stop, accepted ones are paid by the formula.
