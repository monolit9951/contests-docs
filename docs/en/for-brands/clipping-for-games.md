## Which clips sell a game

Trailers announce a game; moments sell it. Five formats work consistently on game tasks; name them in the brief so nobody invents a sixth.

- **The gameplay moment.** One 15-to-30-second episode: a boss, a race, a rare drop, action from the first frame.
- **The fail or the clutch.** A wipe on the last second or a win with one hit point left: the most reposted format, because viewers see themselves in it.
- **How to beat it.** A short guide to the spot where everyone gets stuck; it picks up search and lives for months.
- **The streamer reaction.** A slice of a stream with a real reaction; needs partner streams and the right to cut them.
- **Before and after an update.** Old version against new: graphics, balance, content. The format for patches and seasons.

One format per task is easier to moderate than five; for all of them, run several tasks and compare the views.

## Materials for clippers

A clipper will not buy the game for a task: everything a clip needs sits in one folder linked from the brief and opens without an access request.

1. **A build or access.** Keys for PC and consoles, the store link for a mobile game, a test account with content unlocked; limit keys per creator.
2. **Gameplay captures.** Ten to twenty minutes of clean 1080p footage without overlays or watermarks.
3. **Partner streams.** Links to streams that may be cut and the streamer's written permission; without it a copyright claim takes the clip down with its views.
4. **Logo, name, link.** How the title is spelled, which icon goes in the frame, which link goes in the description.
5. **Spoiler and version rules.** Which scenes stay hidden until a date and which version counts as current.

All five map onto the Materials, Must-be-in-the-clip and Not-allowed fields of the [clipping brief template](/en/for-brands/clipping-brief-template).

## Pay per view or pay per install

The primary mode for a game is pay per view: you set a rate per 1,000 views, a threshold and a cap per clip, and fund the budget. The clipper publishes on their own TikTok, Reels or Shorts account and sends the link; the platform reads views from the site's public counter, reconciles several times a day and drops inflated views.

In open tasks rates currently run from $1 to $2 per 1,000 views, typically $1. The threshold defaults to 1,000 views and is usually 2,000; a clip below it earns nothing, a clip above it is paid for its whole counter. The cap per clip is usually $100, so no single viral clip takes the whole pool. A hypothetical example:

> 20,000 / 1,000 × $1.00 = **$20.00**

The second mode suits games that earn from paying players and need installs rather than reach: a CPA offer pays a flat fee per accepted clip plus a share of purchases by players who came through the creator's tagged link or promo code; conversions arrive by S2S postback, incentivized traffic is banned. The offer for apps is described on the [apps vertical](/en/for-business/apps).

## Launch, update, season: three campaign scenarios

The scenarios use the formula at $1 per 1,000 views and a $100 cap per clip. This is arithmetic, not a forecast: the number of clips and views depends on the game, the materials and the brief.

| Scenario | Pool | Paid views at $1 | Clips at the $100 maximum | The brief |
|---|---:|---:|---:|---|
| Launch | $800 | 800,000 | 8 | moments and fails, deadline two weeks after release |
| Update | $300 | 300,000 | 3 | before-and-after of one patch, one-week deadline |
| Season | $500 a month | 500,000 a month | 5 | a new task every season, how-to guides and partner streams |

**Launch.** The largest pool and two formats at once; hand out materials before release under a publication-date embargo, so clips land in the first week.

**Update.** A small pool and one format; keep the cap at the low end, or three clips take the whole pool.

**Season.** A series of tasks instead of one big one; creators who earned last season come back first.

## What to ban in the brief

A ban missing from the brief cannot be applied afterwards, so the ban list for a game is longer than for any other product.

- **Cheats, exploits and third-party software.** A clip with a cheat sells the cheat, not the game.
- **Leaks.** Datamined content, unannounced scenes, endings before the date in the materials.
- **Toxicity.** Insults aimed at players, developers or competitors, harassment by nickname, mocking newcomers.
- **Other brands and music.** Other games' logos in the frame and music without rights, which the site will mute.
- **False promises.** "Free gems", "paid for watching", giveaways you are not running.
- **Other people's clips.** A re-upload under your hashtag is rejected without pay.

A submission enters the task only through the creator's link and a moderator's check; a clip that breaks a ban is rejected in the selection window and is not paid.

## How to read the results

A campaign has three sources of data, and none replaces the others.

**Counted views.** Each submission shows the link, the counted views and the amount earned. Look at the spread, not the total: five clips at the cap and forty below the threshold means the format was clear to few.

**Rejected submissions.** The rejection rate measures the brief, not the creators: more than a third rejected usually means a vague must-be-in-the-clip list or a ban nobody noticed.

**The store side.** Installs and purchases are not counted by the platform: watch organic installs by day in the store console, clicks on tagged links and promo code activations; on a CPA offer, conversions per creator arrive by postback.

You do not have to hunt for clippers: the task goes into the feed, where creators worldwide see it. ## Frequently asked questions

### Can clipping start before the game is released?

Yes, with materials: closed-beta captures, keys under NDA or pre-recorded gameplay. State the date before which a clip may not be published; a clip published early is rejected.

### What about clips where the creator criticizes the game?

An honest review with complaints does not break the brief unless it contains something banned: toxicity, cheats, other brands. Viewers read such clips as genuine; if criticism is unacceptable, put a specific scenario into the must-be-in-the-clip list instead.

### Does the platform count installs of the game?

No. The platform counts views from the site's public counter and pays by the formula; installs and purchases live in the store console and behind tagged links, and on a CPA offer conversions arrive by postback.

### How many keys should clippers get?

One per creator for the main platform, plus a reserve for consoles if needed. Hand keys out on request through the task, not as an open list: an open list is gone within an hour without a single clip.
