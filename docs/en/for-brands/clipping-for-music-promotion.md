Promoting a song with clippers is simple: you hand them the track and a 15 to 30 second snippet, set a rate per 1,000 views, and they shoot clips to your sound on their own accounts. You pay for counted views, not for posting.

## What artists and labels ask clippers for

Five formats get ordered around a track, each with its own job. List which are accepted, or only the easiest will come.

| Format | What the clipper does | Job in the release | Condition in the brief |
|---|---|---|---|
| Sound under a clip | puts the track under their own story | sound uses and reach | "any story, the whole chorus must play" |
| Lip sync | sings or mouths a line to camera | the lyric and the artist's face stick | "chorus line lip-synced, no intro card" |
| Dance | a routine to the snippet | a trend people copy without a brief | "routine to the snippet, release hashtag in the caption" |
| Concert footage | edits your live material to the studio sound | ticket sales for the tour | "footage from the folder only, city and date on screen" |
| Video teaser | cuts frames from the video to the hook | warm-up before the premiere | "up to 20 seconds, last frame with the premiere date" |

Teaser and concert clips need your footage; the other three the clipper shoots alone, so lip-sync and dance tasks fill fastest. Where the first creators come from: [how to find clippers for your content](/en/for-brands/how-to-find-clippers).

## How to write a brief around a track

**Materials.** The full track, a 15 to 30 second snippet of the catchiest part, the cover art and, if the track is already on the platform, a link to its sound page, so the clips gather under one sound rather than ten uploads. A few frames from the video, not the whole file.

**Required in the clip.** Your sound audible for the whole clip, not two seconds at the end; the release hashtag first in the caption; the artist's name on screen or in the caption. The moderator checks these three points, and they let you reject a clip.

**Not allowed.** Another sound over yours, clips published before release day, insults or other brands next to the artist's name, sped-up versions if you do not want them. A ban missing from the brief cannot be applied after the fact.

Rate, threshold and cap go into separate fields; everything above maps onto the [clipping brief template](/en/for-brands/clipping-brief-template).

## Pay per view for music

Labels count uses of a sound, but the platform counts views of a clip: it reads the public counter of the clip whose link the creator sent. Other people's clips under your sound cost you nothing.

You set three numbers. Open tasks currently pay $1 to $2 per 1,000 views, and music tends to sit at the top of the band because a clip to a sound is quick to shoot. The threshold, typically 2,000 views, drops clips with no reach at no cost. The cap per clip, typically $100, stops one viral clip from taking the budget.

A hypothetical example: a clip reaches 25,000 views at a $2.00 rate.

> 25,000 / 1,000 × $2.00 = **$50.00**

A clip with 80,000 views would give $160.00, but with a $100 cap the creator gets $100.

A release-week budget is a scenario, not a forecast. Say the target is 300,000 counted views in seven days at $2.00: 300,000 / 1,000 × $2.00 = **$600.00**, plus 10 USDT to create the contest; the contest fee is 0%. With a $100 cap that is at least six clips, in practice dozens. A wallet-backed budget is locked on the platform before the start, spent only on counted views, and the remainder comes back. ## Rights and platforms

The rights to the track belong to the buyer: in the brief you confirm you own the recording and lyrics or hold a licence to promote them, or the platform may mute the sound and the views stop. The clipper may use the track only in a clip for this task, published on their own account, where it stays after the campaign.

The platforms are TikTok, Reels and Shorts; which count goes into the terms, and views are read from the public counter of the platform where the clip went out. If you need all three, check each library has the sound: a lip sync to a sound missing from Reels is shot with file audio and never lands on the sound page. Moving the terms into the form: [how to set up a clipping contest](/en/for-brands/set-up-a-clipping-contest).

## How to measure the result

**Counted views.** The platform counts them per clip, and they are the only metric you pay for. Fake views are filtered out; a submission enters the task only after its link is sent and checked by a moderator.

**Growth in sound uses.** Read it on the platform's own sound page, which counts every clip made to the track, including those outside the task. Release week against the week before is the reach effect.

**Streaming clicks.** The platform does not count clicks, so make a tagged release link a required caption field: your analytics then show which clips brought listeners. Terms for labels and distributors: the [business page](/en/for-business).

## Frequently asked questions

### Can I launch the task before the track is released?

Yes, with a snippet and a date. State in the terms the first day clips are accepted: work published earlier is rejected, and release week starts with clips ready.

### Does the clipper have to upload the track to the platform?

No. The rights holder puts the sound on the platform through a distributor; the clipper picks it from the library. If it is missing, the creator uses file audio and the clip never lands on the sound page.

### What if a clip got views but the sound in it was muted?

Check whose sound it is. If the platform muted your track over rights, ask your distributor. If the creator swapped your track for another, the clip fails the point "your sound audible for the whole clip" and is rejected without pay.

### Does this work for an independent artist without a label?

Yes. Launch costs 10 USDT, you set the budget, and spend follows counted views only, so a small pool with a low cap is enough for a first release. Creators worldwide see the task; the largest community is in the CIS.
