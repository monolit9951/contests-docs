A podcast is the easiest source for clips: an hour of conversation already holds finished points, disagreements and answers, and faces on camera give a clip what a screen recording cannot. One episode becomes 10 to 20 clips for TikTok, Reels and Shorts, paid by counted views, not by editing.

## Why a podcast is ideal material for clips

**Hours of talk.** An episode holds 40 to 90 minutes of speech, and the clipper shoots nothing: they find a fragment, add subtitles and put a hook in the first two seconds.

**Finished points.** The guest has already put a thought into a complete sentence and the host has asked the question the audience cares about. The clipper writes no script; they choose the best 20 to 40 seconds.

**Faces on camera.** Two people, a reaction, a pause before the answer. A face holds attention longer than text on a background, and the viewer remembers the host.

## Three clip formats from one episode

| Format | What is in it | Length | Condition in the terms |
|---|---|---|---|
| Quotable point | one complete thought, subtitles, name and credentials on screen | 20 to 40 seconds | "one thought per clip, guest's name in the first second" |
| Disagreement or emotion | pushback, laughter, surprise, both reactions in frame | 15 to 30 seconds | "reaction as the hook, do not cut the other side's answer" |
| Question and answer | the host's question on screen, the answer in full | 30 to 60 seconds | "question on screen, no cuts inside the answer" |

A point gets quoted, a disagreement gets watched to the end, and a question and answer sends people to the full episode. Ask for several clips of each format: one episode is rarely strong in all three.

## What to hand to clippers

**The recording.** The episode video in original quality, via a link that opens without an access request. For an audio-only podcast, the file and the cover art: the clipper builds a clip with a waveform and subtitles, but even one camera brings more views.

**Timecodes, optional.** Five to seven moments you consider strong. They speed up the work, but do not limit creators to them: an outside eye finds what you missed.

**Subtitles.** Required, because most clips are watched on mute. State the language and ask creators to check names and terms.

**Rules for mentioning the guest.** Name and credentials on screen, an episode link in the caption, topics that are off limits if the guest asked. All of it fits the [clipping brief template](/en/for-brands/clipping-brief-template); rate, threshold and cap are separate fields.

## How many clips one episode yields and what it costs

Hypothetically, clippers made 15 clips from an hour-long episode. Three missed the 2,000-view threshold and are not paid; twelve gathered 120,000 counted views at a $1.00 rate.

> 120,000 / 1,000 × $1.00 = **$120.00**

If one clip takes off to 150,000 views, the formula gives $150.00, but with a $100 cap the creator gets $100. At $2.00 the same episode would cost $240.00: open tasks currently pay $1 to $2 per 1,000 views, and the rate is yours. ## Guest, rights and reputation

**The guest's consent.** Agree before release that fragments with them will become short clips on clippers' accounts: one line in the email is enough. A topic the guest wants left out goes into the brief's bans, not a verbal request.

**Rights to the recording.** Yours as the publisher. In the brief, allow the fragments only in clips for this task: the clip goes out on the creator's account and stays there, so write the reposting terms in advance.

**Context.** Ban edits that change the meaning: a question from one guest with another's answer, a sentence cut off mid-way, a title that puts words in the guest's mouth. Such a clip is rejected and not paid, but only if the ban is written down.

**Bans.** Insults aimed at named third parties, the guest's medical or financial advice without their caveats, other products advertised in frame. A submission enters the task only after its link is sent, and the moderator checks both lists.

## How to make clipping a regular part of every episode

Clips work when they go out every week with the episode, not once a season.

1. **Recording day.** The recording goes into the task folder, the guest's name and any timecodes into the description.
2. **The next two days.** Clippers cut and publish; if the episode has a premiere, state the day before which clips are not accepted.
3. **The rest of the week.** Clips gather views, you accept links in the selection window and note which formats worked.
4. **The next recording.** A new episode into the same folder; the budget is topped up and the deadline extended: one task lives all season, and contest creation is paid once.

The best clips become announcements on your own accounts, and the accepted submissions become your Shorts library. ## Frequently asked questions

### Does an audio-only podcast work?

Yes. Hand over the audio file, the cover art and a photo of the guest if you have one: the clipper builds a clip with subtitles and a waveform. Such clips usually get fewer views than faces, so turn on at least one camera.

### How many episodes do I need to launch a task?

One is enough to test the formats and the rate. Regularity matters more than volume: creators who took the first episode return if the second lands in the same folder a week later.

### Can a guest demand that clips be taken down after publication?

The clip lives on the clipper's account, so agree with the guest before release, not after. Topics the guest has closed go into the brief's bans: a clip with them is rejected and not paid.

### How do I get clicks to the full episode if the platform counts only views?

Make the episode link a required caption field and give each creator a tag. The platform counts the views; clicks show up in your analytics by tag, so you see which format brings listeners.
