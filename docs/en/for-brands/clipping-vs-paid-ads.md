## What each dollar buys

In an ad account a dollar buys an impression: the ad appeared, the auction charged you, and whether anyone watched is a separate metric. Above the CPM sit costs no benchmark includes: the shoot, the edit, a media buyer or agency, failed tests.

In clipping a dollar buys a view of a video a creator edited and posted on their own TikTok, Reels or Shorts account. The platform counts views from the network's public counter, filters out inflated numbers, and a clip enters the task only after the creator sends the link and moderation checks it. Reach is organic, recommendations rather than an auction; the clip keeps collecting views after the campaign, and the viewer sees a person's recommendation, not a sponsored post.

## Where paid ads win

Honestly: four jobs stay with paid ads.

- **Precise targeting.** City-level geography, age, interests, lookalikes from your customer list; clips reach the creator's audience plus what the algorithm adds.
- **Speed and scale.** A campaign starts within an hour and buys what the budget allows; clips take days to be made, published and watched.
- **Predictability.** The ad account forecasts reach from the bid; with clips you know the price per thousand, not how many clips will come or which will take off.
- **Conversion measurement.** Pixel, events, click attribution; clip conversions run through a tagged link per creator, promo codes and an S2S postback on CPA tasks.

## Where clips win

- **Price of the result.** Compare a task's rate with spend in an actual ad campaign. Clip views and ad impressions are different units; cost per customer action is more useful for a budget decision.
- **Creative for free.** Creators edit their own clips; on tasks with ready-made videos the platform hands them files with built-in uniqueization, so nobody edits.
- **Trust.** A person's account, no sponsored label, no banner blindness.
- **The long tail.** Ads stop with the budget; clips stay up and keep collecting views.
- **Pay for results.** You pay for views above the threshold on the card by formula, not for intent: 20,000 / 1,000 × $1.00 = **$20.00** for a clip, never more than the cap you set.
- **A wallet-backed budget is locked and spent as it happens.** It sits on the platform before the start, goes out per counted view, and the remainder comes back<!-- source: https://darebay.com/en/about/payout-guarantee 2026-09-24 -->. Launching a task is free, the contest commission is 0%. ## How to combine them

Run a test with materials, a rate stated on the card, threshold, cap and limited budget. Then study the actual clips and their statistics to identify hooks and formats that attracted views. Neither a test period nor a planned submission count guarantees reach.

Ads second: scale the winners where targeting and speed matter. A creator's clip goes into an ad only with their consent; the format, the first three seconds and the copy carry over freely. Writing the task: [commissioning clips for a campaign](/en/for-brands/commission-clips-for-a-campaign); terms for buyers: [/en/for-business](/en/for-business).

## How the comparison was built

External CPMs in the table are dated benchmarks read on 4 September 2026 in each source's currency. They are not prices for your ad account in October: auctions depend on audience and objective. Take DareBay rate and cap from a selected task rather than a general range in an old review.

What cannot be compared head-on:

- **An impression is not a view.** Ad CPM counts ad appearances; DareBay pays for clip views from the public counter, only above the threshold.
- **Reach is not conversion.** Neither number says how many viewers bought; ads measure that with a pixel, clips with tagged links and promo codes.
- **Different markets.** Dollar CPMs are the priciest English-speaking markets, rouble CPMs Russian platforms; on DareBay a task pays the same rate to every creator who takes it, and there is no country list: age requirements and sanctions restrictions apply under the Terms<!-- source: https://darebay.com/en/legal/terms 2026-09-24 -->.
- **CPM is media only.** Creative, management and testing sit outside the benchmarks and inside the price of clips.

## Compare two tests on the same customer action

Use the same product, period and destination page. Record ad spend and creative preparation for ads; creator payments and material preparation for clipping. Count visits and orders in your own analytics using separate tags or codes where the selected network supports them.

Compare cost per confirmed order or qualified inquiry. A clip's public view and an ad impression are different events; cheaper views alone do not establish cheaper sales.

## Frequently asked questions

### Why not just divide the ad CPM by the clipping rate?

Because they count different things: a thousand ad appearances against a thousand views of a clip on a creator's account. An impression counts the moment the ad renders; a view is read from the public counter and paid only above the threshold named in the task. Compare the cost of the result you need: a watch, a click or a sign-up.

### How much budget does a fair test of both channels take?

Launching a task is free, and the budget goes per counted view: hypothetically, ten clips at 20,000 views each at $1.00 cost 10 × 20,000 / 1,000 × $1.00 = **$200.00**, none of them anywhere near a cap. At Meta's $13.48 average CPM <!-- source: https://www.adamigo.ai/blog/meta-ads-cpm-benchmarks-by-industry-2026 2026-09-04 --> the same $200 buys roughly 14,800 impressions, creative not included.

### Do clips replace ads or add to them?

They add, and usually go first: clips cover cheap reach, creative testing and trust, ads cover targeting, speed and scale. Clips alone replace ads where you need mass reach without strict geography: a release, a course, an app, a stream.

### How do I count sales from clips without a pixel?

Through the clip and its caption: a tagged link per creator, promo codes, and on CPA tasks an S2S postback with a fixed fee per accepted clip plus a share of sales. The platform counts the views; sales land in your analytics under the creator's tag.
