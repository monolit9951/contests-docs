Clippers come from four places: your own chat and Discord, freelance boards, your own clip channel, or a pay-per-view platform where creators take the task themselves. The first three cost you time; the fourth moves the counting to the platform.

## Four ways to find clippers

**Your chat and Discord.** The fastest start: a message in chat, a pin in Discord, a story. Your own viewers come and know the tone. Everything after that runs on messaging: agreeing a rate, collecting links, counting views from screenshots, paying each person.

**Freelance boards.** Editors with portfolios and ratings: a plus when you need one strong person for a specific format. They are paid per clip or per hour, for the work rather than reach, so a clip with a hundred views is your risk.

**Your own clip channel with a team.** You open a highlights account, recruit two or three clippers and pay a revenue share or a flat fee. Control is total, but you become a channel manager.

**A pay-per-view platform.** You publish a task, hand over recordings, set a rate per 1000 views and fund a budget. Clippers take it themselves, publish on their own accounts and send in links; the platform counts the views.

| Way | Your time | Control | You pay for | Main risk |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Chat and Discord | a lot: messaging and counting | medium | whatever you agreed | arguments over numbers |
| Freelance board | medium: selection and review | high at the door | a clip or an hour | a clip, not reach |
| Own clip channel | a lot: managing a team | total | revenue share or flat fee | you manage, not stream |
| Pay-per-view platform | little: terms and links | through the task terms | counted views only | clips miss the threshold, but nothing is spent |

## What a clipper needs from you

A good clipper starts the same day if six things are in place.

1. **Access to recordings.** Links to VODs, an archive or a cloud folder; subscriber-only recordings need separate access.
2. **Rights.** Explicit permission to take fragments of your streams and publish them on other accounts.
3. **A hashtag or tag.** How you find the clips and tell your clippers from random ones.
4. **A format.** Vertical 9:16, length, sites (TikTok, Reels, Shorts), and what must not be cut: sponsor integrations, private conversations.
5. **A rate.** How much and for what: per view, per clip or a revenue share.
6. **Regularity.** How many streams a week and when recordings appear.

## Pay per view instead of a flat fee

A flat fee per clip is fair while clips perform evenly. Once one gets 200 thousand views and another 300, the first creator feels cheated and you overpaid. Pay per view removes the argument: earnings grow with reach, and you pay for the result.

On DareBay, across open tasks the rate currently runs from $1.00 to $2.00 per 1000 views. The default threshold is 1000 views, the typical one in open tasks 2000: a clip below it costs nothing. The cap per submission, typically $100, protects the budget from a single viral clip. A wallet-backed budget is locked on the platform before launch and is spent only on counted views; the remainder returns to you. Views are read from the site's public counter, and fraud is filtered out (platform data, snapshot 2026-08-23).

A hypothetical example: a clip got 20 000 views at $1.00.

> 20 000 / 1000 × $1.00 = **$20.00**

The threshold is not deducted: clear it and the whole counter is paid. At 150 000 views the formula would give $150.00, but a $100 cap limits the payout. ## How to launch a task in one evening

1. **Gather the material.** Links to VODs or a folder with the latest streams.
2. **Write the terms.** Format, sites, hashtag, what must not be cut, how many clips you accept per creator.
3. **Set the budget.** Rate per 1000 views, threshold, cap, total. Contest creation costs 10 USDT once, the contest fee is 0%, topping up is 0%.
4. **Publish.** The task appears in the feed and creators take it. Step by step: [how to set up a clipping contest](/en/for-brands/set-up-a-clipping-contest); other formats are on the [business page](/en/for-business).
5. **Accept links.** A submission counts only after its link is sent and a moderator checks it against the terms; unfit clips are rejected and unpaid.
6. **Payout.** In wallet-backed mode each amount follows the formula from counted views; with manual payout you pay from the platform's statement, in Stars or to a TON wallet.

## What to do with the finished clips

**Usage rights.** State in the task that you may repost finished clips and use them in promos. The creator accepted the terms before submitting.

**Reposts.** The best clips go into stories, the pinned post and the next stream's announcement.

**Your own highlights channel.** Links to accepted submissions stay in the task, so a highlights channel assembles itself: the clip channel from the first section, with a team already paid per view.

## Frequently asked questions

### Do I need a big channel for clippers to take my content?

No. A clipper cares about the material and the terms, not your channel size. A task on the platform is seen by creators worldwide, the largest community being in the CIS, and taken by those who like the rate and format.

### Can I pay clippers directly, without the platform wallet?

Yes, that is manual payout mode: the platform counts views and draws up the statement, and you pay the creators yourself, in Stars or to a TON wallet. No money enters the platform, so creators have no guarantee.

### What if a clipper posted a clip but never sent the link?

A submission counts only after its link is sent; the platform does not collect clips by hashtag. Remind the creator while the task is open: without a link the clip is not counted.

### Who owns the clips, and can I post them on my own channel?

The clip goes out on the creator's account; the source footage is yours. Put the usage terms into the task: reposts, promos, compilations. A clipper who submitted work accepted them.
