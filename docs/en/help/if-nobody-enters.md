# What if nobody enters?

You lose nothing but the time you spent writing it. A contest that closes with zero valid entries
hands a wallet-backed budget straight back to your available balance, leaves your organizer rating
untouched and asks nothing of you to make that happen. What is worth reading next is which budget
comes back and which was never held in the first place.

## What happens to the budget

In a **wallet-backed contest** the money was charged from your available balance at creation and
tied to that contest, so it returns to that balance in full when the contest reaches its final
state. Nothing to file, nothing to chase.

A contest **without a locked pool** is different: the platform never held anything, so there is no
refund to queue. You simply never paid.

Item prizes were never physically held by DareBay either, so there is nothing to
return; you simply keep the item.

## What happens to fees

Creating a contest and topping up its budget currently carry a **0% fee** across fiat, USDT and
COINS, so there is no separate “processing share” or “feed-placement share” to refund. The final
report shows the unused budget returned. Store purchases and a later balance withdrawal are
different operations with their own [current terms](/en/help/what-commission).

## Your rating does not drop

Zero submissions does not hurt your **organizer rating**. It happens for
honest reasons: a narrow topic, a short deadline, a prize that does not
match the effort. The rating only moves when something went wrong *through
your own fault* (you did not pay out the prize, you changed the rules
mid-contest, and so on).

## How to avoid it

- **Give people time.** Under 24 hours for anything non-trivial is too
  little.
- **Match the prize to the effort.** A 5-minute selfie for $500 will draw a
  crowd; a 10-hour video for $20 will not.
- **Be specific in the terms.** "Best content" gets you spam; "a
  30-second vertical video of X in the style of Y" gets you real
  submissions.
- **Pick the right category.** Narrow categories have a smaller audience,
  but a relevant one.
